Welte-Mignon T-100 · roll 3309

Reading the Nuancierbalg

Roll 3309 carries both halves of a problem on the same sheet: two hand-drawn Handnuancierung lines down the middle of the paper, and the punched dynamic code a Welte piano would actually play from. That makes it possible to check an expression emulator against something, which is normally the difficulty. These are the measurements that came out of the comparison, and what they say about the two existing emulators.

What is being compared

A few late red Welte rolls carry two lines drawn by hand down the centre of the paper, under a printed header reading Handnuancierung · Bass. · Discant and a five-line scale marked P.P. · M.F. · F.F. · M.F. · P.P. Pfeffer's dissertation reads them as a record of the two Nuancierbälge, set ahead of the punches „wohl um der menschlichen Reaktionszeit bei der Handnuancierung Rechnung zu tragen" (Fn. 50), citing Gottschewski's Sydney paper of 2024 for the first half of that. Gottschewski's published work does not discuss these lines at all; what he argues in print is that the dynamics must lead the notes for a mechanical reason, since the recorder registers striking force at the attack and the level has to be reached before it. The reaction-time reading is therefore Pfeffer's, and the two explanations are not the same. The tracer in this repository turns them into numbers, one sample per pixel row of the Stanford scan: 198 630 rows over 5:26, of which about 93 % are sightings of ink and the rest interpolation across punched holes, printed gridlines and gaps. Everything below is masked to the sightings.

The other half is the punched code on the same scan, read from the expression tracks of Stanford's raw MIDI. 2 763 perforations, six functions per keyboard half. An emulator consumes those and should produce the drawn line. Two exist: Stanford's midi2exp (Shi and Sapp) and its port in SUL-CIDR's pianolatron. Both integrate a constant rate.

One thing is worth being exact about, because it changes what a good fit would mean. The punches were derived from the line, not the other way round. So a model fitted here is inverting the punching, and it reproduces a playback Welte only to the extent that whoever punched the roll was working in the mechanism's own terms — which the results below suggest they were, since the code turns out to carry things like a hard stop and an exponential approach that only a mechanism would put there. Hagmann has nothing on Welte's piano recording apparatus and reports that the procedure was never documented, so the step from this roll to an instrument remains an inference.

What the roll says

Confirms Gottschewski, with a number

The drawn line runs ahead of the punches by about a tenth of a second

Measured from the sharp collapse of the line at each sforzando release: 108.6 ms in the bass (95 % CI 106.3–110.0, interquartile range 9.9 ms, n = 227) and 75.7 ms in the treble (72.9–79.3, IQR 17.2, n = 274). An independent estimate, scanning for the offset that best separates latch-on from latch-off slope across the whole roll, gives the same sign and agrees to within 14 and 26 ms.

An interquartile range of 10 ms on 227 events is a tight relation, and the obvious mundane explanation has been ruled out rather than assumed. Where a punch removes the paper under the drawn line the tracer flags the rows, which gives 282 and 255 places where the two coordinate systems can be compared directly. The gap in the traced line sits inside the punch, inset at both ends by equal amounts, and its midpoint falls on the punch's midpoint to within one scan row, about 1.7 ms. The two systems share an origin, and the lead is 40 to 60 times larger than the residual uncertainty in it.

Against both emulators

The bellows does not move at a constant rate

Plotting the rate of travel against position, rather than against time, separates the candidate laws cleanly. In every valve state the rate falls roughly linearly with position, which is what an exponential relaxation towards an asymptote gives and what a constant rate cannot. For the slow decrescendo the exponential fit has a weighted rms of 0.019 against 0.120 for a constant, and the picture is the same in the treble.

The time constants are not close to the published ones either. The fast decrescendo crosses the whole stop-to-stop span in 55 ms (bass) and 62 ms (treble), against midi2exp's 400 ms — a factor of six to seven. The fast crescendo, at 211 and 257 ms, is of the right order as its 300 ms. The slow decay differs in shape as well as in speed.

Confirms Welte's own regulation

Opening and closing take the same time, as the service instructions require

Hagmann reports that the throttled bore 100 exists precisely so that the bellows reopens in the same elapsed time as it took to close, and Welte's scale-roll procedure tests it directly: the bellows must travel from piano to mezzoforte and back „wobei Oeffnung und Schliessung in gleicher Geschwindigkeit zu erfolgen haben". That regulation is visible in the drawn line. The two directions have quite different time constants, 1168 ms against 592 ms in the bass, because they relax towards different asymptotes; the traversal times come out at 1072 and 1211 ms.

Against the printed scale

The Mezzoforte hook does not sit on the M.F. gridline

When the hook is set the line piles up sharply at one level, and that level is not the printed M.F. line. It is 0.5752 in the bass and 0.6169 in the treble: 0.076 and 0.117 above the gridline, and 0.62 to 0.64 of the way from the piano stop to the forte stop. The forte stop itself falls short of the printed F.F. line by 0.088 and 0.048, while the piano stop sits essentially on the P.P. line.

The level is repeatable to one or two pixels within a single engagement, standard deviation 0.002 to 0.004, and drifts a little more than that between engagements. That drift is not the hook moving. The fortissimo stop, which is a mechanical limit no hook can reach, drifts by the same amount in the same direction: across the nine holds where both are readable the two correlate at 0.90 in the bass and 0.97 in the treble, with near-equal spreads. Two independent stops cannot move in lockstep, so what is moving is the whole traced line against the printed gridlines. Referred to the fortissimo stop of its own hold, the hook is steady to 0.0013, and sits at 0.627 and 0.645 of the travel from the piano stop to the forte stop, with a standard deviation of 0.002 to 0.003. That fraction is the stable form of the number, and it is a quarter of the way above the midpoint the printed scale claims.

The tracer cannot tell the drawn line from a printed gridline, so a stop sitting exactly on the M.F. line would be invisible and might show up as a spurious mode just above it. That was tested for and it is not what is happening. Across 180 676 rows with the hook set, two carry the flag that marks the gridline; the hook-engaged stretches are the cleanest part of the whole trace, not the most degraded. The excluded band measures 0.487 to 0.513, five to eight times its own half-width below the stops. And the decisive comparison: in the band from 0.513 to 0.55, which is clear of the exclusion and perfectly readable, there are no rows at all while the hook is set, against 4 503 bass and 5 402 treble rows there when it is released, 94 % of them plain ink. The tracer reads that band whenever the line goes there. With the hook set the line never goes there.

What that level is a measurement of needs care. The pin has extent along the direction the bellows travels, so it does not arrest it at one level but at two: a board descending onto it rests against its upper face, a board rising onto it against its lower face, and the band between cannot be occupied while the pin is down. Every one of the twenty-two engagements on this roll is an arrival from above, so 0.576 and 0.617 are the upper face. Of 289 bass and 256 treble settled stretches inside the holds, 150 and 113 are clear arrivals from above and none is a clear arrival from below, which is the same absence, from the same cause, as the missing crossings. The pin's centre lies half a thickness below the measured face and the roll cannot say how far.

So the offsets from the printed gridline are an upper bound on how far the hook was set above the midpoint, not an estimate of it — and the drawing says the bound is nearly all of it. Read at 211 dpi from the plate, the stop is not the open notch it had been taken for. The arm of the Mezzoforte bellows ends in a plain blunt downward finger, and the nuance bellows' board carries a small upstanding pin facing it; both plates draw the same pair. A finger arrests the pin from whichever side the pin is on when it comes down, so there are two arrest levels separated by one finger plus one pin, and they differ in the bellows' own coordinate rather than in height.

Measured on the plate, the finger sits at about 53 % of the bellows' full travel, which is Hagmann's „auf halbem Wege" and, near enough, the printed scale's midpoint. The dead band is of order a tenth of the stroke, though a pen stroke on a freehand schematic is not a dimension and the drawing will not support better than an order of magnitude. Arriving always from above, as this roll always does, the line should then rest half a dead band above the finger — and 53 % plus half of a dead band in the range the drawing allows lands on the measured 62.7 % and 64.5 %.

So the mechanism's mezzoforte is at half travel after all, and the drawn line rests above it because of the geometry of how it gets there. The printed M.F. gridline and the mechanism agree; what disagrees with both is the level the line comes to rest at, and that is a consequence of the stop having a thickness. This is the reading I would now put on the anomaly, and it wants a second roll to test, since it predicts that a roll whose editor engaged the hook from below would show the line resting below the printed line by the same amount.

Two readings of the residual offset are open. The hook is an adjustable part — Welte's control 2c has the technician move it to balance bass against treble at mezzoforte — so it may simply have been regulated above half travel on the recording machine. Or the drawn scale is not linear in bellows travel, in which case the printed M.F. line is a claim about loudness and not about position. The two halves differing by more than the tracer's uncertainty is consistent with either, since they share the central F.F. gridline and an error there moves the two scales in opposite directions.

Whether the pin blocks travel one way or both, this roll cannot say, and the reason is worth more than the answer would have been. Hagmann's „Oeffnung oder Schliessung … auf halbem Wege unterbricht" reads as a stop that confines the bellows to whichever half of the travel it is already in; the alternative is a floor it can rise through. The two differ only when the hook engages while the line is below the stop and something then drives it up. That case never arises here. All twenty-two Mezzoforte-on punches fire while the line is standing at the fortissimo stop, at 0.904 to 0.918 in the bass and 0.924 to 0.954 in the treble. The line is established below the stop in none of the eleven bass holds and one of the eleven treble ones.

Counting crossings of the stop with the hook set gives zero in either direction, on a test that could not have detected one. The emulator says the same from the other side: run with the two readings of the pin and everything else held, the model produces bit-identical output over all 198 630 rows. So on this roll the hook is an editorial device for catching a falling line at mezzoforte and is never used to hold a rising one down — eleven engagements per half, median 10 s, longest 27, covering 45.5 % of the drawn line between them — and which kind of stop it is cannot be decided from it.

A property of the linkage, measured

The bellows bounces off the Mezzoforte stop

Where the line falls fast onto the hook it does not simply stop. It goes past the level it comes to rest at and springs back: past the stop by 0.003 to 0.005 within a few milliseconds, back through the resting level by 0.024 in the bass and 0.012 in the treble at 14 to 20 ms, settled by about 50 ms. One cycle of a heavily damped bounce, with the same 32 ms period in both halves — which is what two copies of one linkage should give.

Three things make it a mechanical property rather than an artefact. It survives re-tracing at a fiftieth of the step penalty, agreeing point by point to 0.002, so it is ink and not the tracer rounding a corner. Its period is the same in both halves. And its amplitude grows with the speed of arrival, +0.00055 units per unit per second at the bass hook with an interval excluding zero, which is what a compliant contact must do and a coincidence need not.

So the stop is a stiff spring rather than a wall — a thin bellows leaf carrying a long rod, arrested by a finger, all of it arriving with momentum. The 32 ms period fixes the spring constant and the single-cycle decay fixes the damping, so this is a measurement of the linkage and not two more fitted parameters.

The two rails show no such rebound, and the reason is worth stating because it is not evidence that they are rigid. They are never approached at more than 10 units per second, against 20 and more at the hook, because a fast crescendo rolls off over the last sixth of its travel and the line decelerates into them. The roll therefore cannot say whether the rails are compliant. The hook's own speed dependence, extrapolated down to rail speeds, predicts an overshoot of 0.001 — half a traced pixel — so the question is unanswerable here and also of no consequence.

Against Hagmann, with midi2exp

The sforzando acts per perforation, not as a latch

Pfeffer's Leseregeln already state the rule this way — „wirken Sforzando an und Sforzando ab nur über die Dauer der eigentlichen Stanzung" — without marking that it departs from Hagmann, to whom the chapter refers the mechanism as a whole. What follows is the evidence for that reading rather than a new one.

Hagmann is explicit that the sforzando holds, like the crescendo and the mezzoforte: „Und auch hier wird die Funktion aufrecht erhalten, selbst wenn die entsprechende Perforation verschwindet", and Welte's own control 7 has a sforzando still driving the bellows after its perforation has gone. midi2exp instead reads it as active only while the perforation is under the tracker bar. On this roll midi2exp is right, and the case against the latch is not close.

Fitting decides it first. With everything else free and the search converged, held-out error is 0.044 reading the sforzando per pulse against 0.156 reading it as a latch, and no choice of the valve's target rescues the latched reading — I checked, having first got this wrong by setting a parameter bound that excluded the physically correct value.

Two facts about the punching say the same thing more directly. Read as set-and-cancel, the sforzando would be engaged for 61 % of the roll, which is not what an accent does. And 371 of the 587 bass sforzando-on perforations, 63 %, fall while the latch would already be set: under a latching reading the editor cut several hundred holes that do nothing whatever, while under a per-pulse reading every one of them does work. That is evidence about what the punching expected the mechanism to do.

An earlier reading of the drawn line here appeared to support the latch — after a sforzando rise ends, the line holds instead of falling. That turned out to be a confound. The crescendo latch is itself engaged 63 % of the time, and a latched crescendo alone predicts the hold. Controlling for it leaves six clean bass episodes, and they fall at 0.62 scale units per second, near the 0.98 the per-pulse reading predicts and nowhere near a hold. The treble's apparent hold is a window artefact: it accelerates across the window rather than decaying, which is the next excursion arriving, not a valve holding.

Gottschewski settles it from a different direction entirely, and without discussing the question. Enumerating the six dynamics tracks he writes that the last pair is not an on/off pair at all:

Im Gegensatz zu den ersten beiden Spurpaaren, die jeweils eine Funktion ab- oder anschalten, handelt es sich bei dem letzten Spurpaar um zwei verschiedene Funktionen, die auch gleichzeitig wirken können. Gottschewski 1996, p. 138, n. 108

Mezzoforte and slow crescendo each have an explicit cancelling track; the sudden crescendo has none. Its neighbour is the sudden decrescendo, a second function in its own right, and the two can act at once — which is exactly what an on/off pair for a single latched state cannot do. His arithmetic for the later 98-note scale corroborates it: the two latching pairs collapse to one track each while the last pair keeps both.

Welte's own regulation procedure for the T-100 says it outright. Anhang 12, control 4b:

Im Bass und Diskant gleichzeitig Eingabe von sechs kurzen Perforationen Sforzando an: Die Nuancierbälge 90 müssen sich in sechs Schritten von der Piano- zur Mezzoforte-Stellung bewegen. Nach Rückfall in die Piano-Stellung Eingabe von sechs noch kürzeren Perforationen Sforzando an: Die Nuancierbälge 90 dürfen sich nicht bewegen. — Wird die Mezzoforte-Stellung zu früh oder zu spät erreicht, Korrektur an 20. Hagmann 1984, Anhang 12, after Welte's scale-roll booklet for the T-100

Four things in that one paragraph tell against a latch. Six pulses give six steps, where a latch set by the first would drive continuously. They stop at mezzoforte, so the endpoint depends on how many there were. The bellows falls back to piano with no cancel named, where Hagmann names cancels explicitly in the neighbouring controls. And the correction clause trims how much travel each pulse buys, which under a latch would not be a quantity. Control 6b then describes the editor's practice on roll 3309 exactly: five short on-pulses which, „trotz des automatischen Eintritts der Decrescendo-Bewegung", must keep bringing the bellows back to forte.

Which leaves the question of why Hagmann says the opposite, and the answer is a matter of provenance rather than of reading. His T-100 plate is captioned „Nach Welte 2", and Welte 2 is the Betriebsanleitung for the T-98 — the plate for one instrument is redrawn from the manual of the other. Its three relay "on" units are moreover one hand-drawn module repeated at a fixed pitch, matching each other far better than any arbitrary offset does, so the hold chamber visible on the sforzando unit is not an independent observation of the sforzando unit. Anhang 12, by contrast, rests on Welte's scale-roll booklet for the T-100 itself. Where the two disagree the second is the better witness, and it agrees with the roll.

His prose can then be read without conflict at all. „Aufrecht erhalten" holds equally of the valve staying open and of the bellows merely staying where it got to because nothing returns it; the second is true, and is what this model does.

Against Hagmann, for the punching

The sforzando and crescendo codes are not punched together

Hagmann writes that setting the sforzando always sets the crescendo too, so that the bellows is held closed through both conduits. Whether he means the roll or the relay is ambiguous in the text, and the roll can answer for the punching. Against 2 000 circularly shifted copies of the sforzando series, every co-occurrence excess sits inside the chance interval, and the offset distributions are flat with no peak at zero. If anything the two codes avoid one another: 3.4 % of bass sforzando-on punches have a crescendo-on within 40 rows, against 6.8 % by chance.

That leaves a clean mechanical prediction, and the roll bears it out. Conduit 39 is joined to the bellows permanently and the crescendo relay only switches its far end between blower vacuum and atmosphere, so a sforzando fired with the crescendo cancelled is fighting conduit 39 while one fired with it set has both conduits pulling. The measured difference is 1.15 units/s in the bass and 1.08 in the treble; simply adding the two conductances predicts 0.75 and 0.65, which is 65 % and 60 % of it. The right kind of explanation, and not the whole of it.

A correction to both code maps

Bass lines 9 and 10 are the Widerstand, not the motor

midi2exp and pianolatron both label MIDI keys 22 and 23 as the motor switch. Hagmann's table of the T-100 scale (Anhang 10, p. 178) gives bass lines 9 and 10 as Widerstand ab and an, the two-speed control of the blower. Roll 3309 works them twenty-two times, at musical junctures rather than at the ends of the roll, so they are an expression device. Since the blower feeds the relay's vacuum chamber as well as the note pneumatics, they change how hard the bellows is pulled closed, not only how loud the instrument is.

Settled, on positive evidence

The line records the bellows, not the vacuum it delivers

This is the assumption everything else rests on, and Hagmann's mechanism makes it testable. The regulator bellows senses the vacuum delivered to the note pneumatics and pulls the cone valve open when a chord makes it dip, on the timescale of an attack. So a trace of delivered vacuum must carry note-locked movement, and a trace of bellows position cannot, because the regulator acts on the cone valve and not on the Nuancierbalg.

Aligned on note attacks across the whole roll, the line dips by 2.0 px in the bass and 2.6 in the treble at 28 to 54 ms, four times the spread of shifted controls, reaching 7 and 11 px on chords of five notes or more. Taken alone that looks like the vacuum reading, and it is not a tracing artefact: it is as large for notes whose tracker columns lie well away from the traced band as for those crossing it.

But it is commanded motion. Big chords are where the accents are, so an average aligned on attacks collects the expression excursions that follow them. Excluding rows within 200 ms of any expression event the dip falls to 0.09 and 0.23 px, at or below the control. And in the decisive form, rows where the line stays within 0.02 of the Mezzoforte stop for the whole window so that the bellows is provably against a mechanical stop and cannot move, the note-triggered average is smaller than the shifted controls, 0.8 and 0.7 times, with large chords no different.

The bound on any note-locked feature where the bellows is pinned is 0.40 px in the bass and 0.49 in the treble, about 0.001 of the printed scale. Against the trace's own reproducibility of 0.0003 that is three times the noise, so this is a null and not a failure to resolve. Where the bellows cannot move the line does not move with the notes; where it can, everything note-locked is accounted for by the punched code.

One limit worth keeping: a pen linkage with appreciable inertia would attenuate a 30 ms wiggle, and nothing here measures that. The bound is on what reached the paper, not on what the vacuum did.

The model

The state is the closure of the nuance bellows: 0 fully open, which sets the cone valve for the least vacuum and so the softest attack, 1 fully closed and loudest. Air is drawn out of it or admitted to it through conduits of different bore, and how fast a bellows fills through a conduit depends on how far it still has to go.

dx/dt  =  Σpaths   g · a · sgn(T − x) · |T − x|α g the conductance of the conduit, a how far the tracker port is open, T the position that path pulls towards, α the exponent of the flow law: 1 for a laminar throttle, giving exponential approach; ½ for an orifice, where flow goes as the square root of the pressure difference; 0 for a constant rate, which is what the existing emulators assume. α is fitted, so the family contains them as a special case and the roll decides.

Three things follow from Hagmann's account of the relay and are built in rather than added as rules. Conduit 39 is joined to the bellows permanently and the crescendo relay only switches its far end between blower vacuum and atmosphere, so midi2exp's separate rule that a slow decrescendo always runs is simply the state of that one conduit when the crescendo latch is off. The Mezzoforte pin is a mechanical stop whose position is a regulated setting, so it is fitted rather than fixed at the printed gridline. And the sforzando release acts only while its perforation is open and accumulates across successive punches, which is what Welte's control 4e requires: three short holes must bring the bellows down to piano in three steps.

Two things are modelled that neither existing emulator has. The tracker port opens gradually, because the hole in the paper and the hole in the tracker bar are both round and of comparable size — 20.9 px and 16.7 px here — so the port opens over the roughly 17 px it takes them to slide across each other, some 28 ms at this roll's speed, which is the same order as the mechanism's own fast time constants. The open area is the lens where the two circles overlap. And the Widerstand scales the closing conductances, since the blower feeds the relay.

Errors are in units of the printed scale, where 1.0 is the whole P.P. to F.F. span. Two things the transits demanded

The relay valve does not open in proportion to the tracker port's area. Air enters its membrane chamber through the port and lifts the valve once the chamber has charged past a threshold, and the model had been letting the valve follow the port's own opening, which takes about 25 milliseconds to slide across the bore. That was the whole of its remaining sluggishness on the closing side: averaged over every sforzando, the drawn line reaches its greatest speed at 50 ms and the model at 67 to 83, with the peak heights agreeing. Letting the valve lift over about a sixth of the charge above its threshold instead of the whole of it removes most of the gap. Collapsing it further, to a valve that snaps open, is far worse: the graded opening is load-bearing across the four fifths of the roll that are not transits, and only its last three quarters are not.

The second is a load the nuancing system places on its own blower. With the crescendo relay off, conduit 39 stands open to atmosphere while the sforzando valve draws on wind chamber 15, so air runs straight through the bellows and out again without moving it. That draw grows with how far the bellows sits from its open rest, and it acts on only five to nine per cent of rows, those where a sforzando draws with the crescendo cancelled. It is what makes the model over-drive a sforzando by about a quarter of its excursion when the crescendo is off while being close to right when it is on, an asymmetry the pooled averages hid entirely, because the pooled figure is the mean of a large positive and a small negative.

Both were priced against a control fitted on the same budget, with the terms held at the values that reproduce the model without them, two searches each from different seeds. Held out, the pair is worth 0.0368 to 0.0302 in the bass and 0.0478 to 0.0393 in the treble, against seed spreads of 0.0001 to 0.0028. Two further candidates were tried the same way and rejected: a lift band of its own for the cancelling valve, which loses in the bass and is undecided in the treble, and dry friction in the linkage, which loses in the bass and gains inside the noise in the treble.

The first row is the one worth dwelling on. The rebound at the Mezzoforte stop is measured in the drawn line, with a consistent 32 ms period in both halves, a survival through re-tracing at a fiftieth of the step penalty, and an amplitude that grows with arrival speed. It is also not measurable in the fit: making the stop rigid is better in the bass and worse in the treble by about the width of the search noise. Both of those are true, and a model may reasonably carry a mechanism that is well evidenced in the data even where it buys no accuracy — provided the second half is said as plainly as the first.

One intended diagnostic failed outright. To bound what any model with this state could achieve, the drawn line's own speed was binned by valve state and position and that table run forward as a model. It scores 0.29 and 0.26, at the level of a constant: a velocity field estimated along the true trajectory diverges when run open-loop. That is a fact about free simulation rather than a bound.

Tested against things it was not fitted to

A model with two dozen parameters fitted to one curve can be right for the wrong reasons, and the held-out blocks do not guard against that: they only show it has not memorised the roll. The stronger test is whether it does what the instrument was documented to do, on inputs it never saw. Two sources make statements of that kind. Welte's own regulation procedure for the T-100, which a technician ran against a scale roll and which Hagmann reprints as Anhang 12, and the reading rules in Pfeffer's dissertation. Both describe the mechanism rather than this performance, so a synthetic roll built to their specification tests the model against them directly.

Six such statements can be put to the model, and it satisfies all six. Nothing here was optimised for; two of them caught mistakes.

The third row is worth dwelling on, because it was at first read as a failure. The model stops the bellows below the hook's nominal level, not at it. That is what a finger of finite thickness must do to a bellows arriving from underneath, and it is the same geometry that puts the resting level of a bellows arriving from above a half-thickness too high — which is the whole of the Mezzoforte anomaly. One structure, three consequences, none of them fitted.

How this was checked

Rows the tracer could not witness carry no evidence and are excluded from every number here: where a punch has removed the paper, where the drawn line runs along a printed gridline and cannot be told from it, and where nothing was visible and the path was bridged. That is 6.1 % of bass rows and 6.5 % of treble rows. Nothing is filled in.

The measurements and the emulator were built independently, in Python and in TypeScript, from the same two files, and the two pipelines agree: both find 2 763 expression perforations, matching one for one on code and on both edges to the row, and their time axes agree to 0.05 ms, which is the rounding in the traced file. That rules out a reading error in either as the source of anything above.

The remaining error is the model's, not the tracing's. At the steepest edges the drawn line moves about 16 px sideways per scan row, which raised the worry that the tracer's step penalty was flattening exactly the features the model is judged on. Re-tracing those stretches at a fifth and a fiftieth of the penalty changes nothing: over rows moving faster than 8 units/s the typical disagreement between traces is 0.0003 scale units and the 99th percentile about 0.03, and neither depends on the penalty, so it is run-to-run reproducibility rather than smoothing. Below the accepted penalty the path starts following the edges of punched holes instead of ink, and every one of those departures falls on rows already excluded as unwitnessed. The residual spikes of ±0.3 are an order of magnitude above the floor.

Models are fitted on alternating eight-second blocks and scored on the blocks left out, so a model cannot win on parameter count. Two things that does not show. Each held-out block sits between two blocks the model was fitted on, and the model carries state through both, so the split is a guard against overfitting rather than a demonstration of generalisation. And it is one roll: a constant fitted here is a constant of this reading of this performance on this instrument, and there is no second witness to try it on.

What is not settled

Two of these got worse over the course of the work rather than better, which is the honest direction for an open question to move.

  • The machine that drew this line did not satisfy Welte's own regulation. Its control 6c requires nine sforzando on-off pairs to hold the bellows at mezzoforte, so the two per-pulse increments must be equal and opposite. On this roll the falling one is 3.9 to 4.3 times the rising one, measured directly from the line on 51 and 81 clean episodes with no model involved. The model's four-fold cancelling valve is therefore matching an asymmetry that is in the paper, not compensating for a defect in itself. What that licenses is narrow: the recording apparatus's two fast valves were not balanced the way the playback regulation prescribes. Hagmann reports that apparatus as entirely undocumented, and nothing here says what it was.
  • One roll, one performance, one instrument. Every constant here is a constant of this reading of this performance. The sharpest available test of the Mezzoforte result — a roll whose editor engaged the hook from below, which should show the line resting below the printed gridline by the same amount — needs a second witness, and there is none.
  • Bass and treble disagree on several numbers that ought to match if the halves share a mechanism. Referring the hook to the fortissimo stop removed most of that disagreement, which suggests the rest may also be calibration rather than mechanism, but it has not been separated.
  • Whether the Mezzoforte pin blocks one way or both cannot be decided from this roll: all twenty-two engagements happen with the line already at the fortissimo stop, so the discriminating case never arises, and the two readings produce identical output.
  • The thinnest measurements are the slow decrescendo, which rests on about 1 % of the roll, and the fast crescendo with the crescendo latch off, on nine and fifteen episodes. The fitted decay in fact beats the measured one, which is a sign the measurement is the weaker of the two rather than the model being wrong.
