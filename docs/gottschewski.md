# Gottschewski on the Welte dynamics coding

What Hermann Gottschewski has written that bears on modelling the Nuancierbalg of a T-100 from
the punched expression code. Compiled 2026-08-31.

Short version: one footnote in the 1996 book settles question 1 in your favour, and does so more
cleanly than the fitting evidence does. Nothing published by him touches the drawn nuancing lines
at all. He gives a great deal of quantitative material on the time axis and almost none on the
pneumatics.

## 1. How the sforzando line behaves

**This is settled, and against Hagmann.** The decisive passage is a footnote to the roll-reading
rules, and it is the only place in the book where the six dynamics tracks are enumerated.

> **108** Die sechs Dynamikspuren haben folgende Funktion (von außen nach innen): Mezzofortearre­
> tierung aus – Mezzofortearretierung ein – langsames crescendo aus (d. h. langsames decrescendo) –
> langsames crescendo ein – plötzliches decrescendo – plötzliches crescendo. Im Gegensatz zu den er­
> sten beiden Spurpaaren, die jeweils eine Funktion ab- oder anschalten, handelt es sich bei dem letzten
> Spurpaar um zwei verschiedene Funktionen, die auch gleichzeitig wirken können. Dementsprechend
> war es möglich, für das spätere System (98er, vgl. S. 26, Fußn. 23) die Dynamik auf vier Spuren und
> die beiden Pedale auf je eine zu reduzieren. (Eine Tonspur vereinigt ebenfalls die Funktionen „Ton
> ein" und „Ton aus" auf einer Spur.)
>
> — p. 138, Fn. 108 (verified against a high-resolution render of the page)

In English: the six dynamics tracks, from the outside inwards, are mezzoforte-arrest off,
mezzoforte-arrest on, slow crescendo off (i.e. slow decrescendo), slow crescendo on, sudden
decrescendo, sudden crescendo. *In contrast to the first two track pairs, each of which switches one
function off or on, the last track pair consists of two different functions, which can also act
simultaneously.*

### What this states

1. The "sforzando" line is his *plötzliches crescendo*. It is **not** one half of an on/off pair.
   The mezzoforte arrest has an explicit "off" track and the slow crescendo has an explicit "off"
   track. The sudden crescendo has none.
2. The sudden crescendo and the sudden decrescendo are two **independent** functions that **can act
   at the same time**.
3. His arithmetic for the 98-note system corroborates the reading. Two latching pairs collapse to
   one track each, exactly as a note track carries both "Ton ein" and "Ton aus"; the last pair, being
   two genuine functions, stays on two tracks. 2 + 2 = 4, which is the figure he gives.

### What this implies for the latch question

Under Hagmann's reading the sforzando is maintained after the perforation ends, *until cancelled*.
For that to work there must be a canceller. The only candidate on the roll is the sudden
decrescendo, and Gottschewski says explicitly that the two can act simultaneously, which is what an
on/off pair for a single latched state cannot do. So his description of the track functions is
**incompatible with a latched sforzando state cancelled by its neighbour**. That is my inference,
stated as such — he never uses the word *Sforzando*, never mentions Hagmann's claim, and nowhere
discusses latching.

There is a real ambiguity in "maintained" that is worth separating out, because part of the dispute
may be verbal. Three readings:

- (a) the valve latches, the *rate* continues to be applied after the perforation has gone;
- (b) the valve is momentary, the rate is applied only while the perforation is open, but the
  bellows then **stays where it got to**, because nothing returns it;
- (c) the valve is momentary and the effect decays.

Gottschewski's account is consistent with (b) and inconsistent with (a). Your emulator's
"acting only while its perforation is under the tracker bar" is (b) if the integrator holds its
state, which is presumably what it does. Hagmann's "maintained after the perforation has gone" may
have been a loose description of (b) rather than a claim of (a). Your held-out error of 0.045 against
0.156 discriminates (a) from (b) empirically, and Gottschewski discriminates them textually, in the
same direction.

### Supporting passage on the rate model

Also from Gottschewski, on the same principle:

> Die Regelung der Anschlagsstärke erfolgt nach einem dynamischen Prinzip, d. h. in der Regel (außer
> bei der geringsten, der höchsten und zu Steuerungszwecken einer mittleren Lautstärke) verändert
> sich die die Dynamik regulierende Saugspannung kontinuierlich. Es gibt also nicht verschiedene
> Lautstärkestufen, sondern verschiedene Crescendo- und Decrescendostufen, zwischen denen mittels
> der Steuerspuren umschaltbar ist.
>
> — p. 28

There are no loudness *steps*, only crescendo and decrescendo *rates*, between which the control
tracks switch. That is the model you are fitting, stated by him in 1996.

### What he does not say

- He **never** uses the word *Sforzando*. There is no index entry for it (it would fall between
  "Serkin, Rudolf 72" and "Singen 176, 232" on p. 329, and does not).
- He **never** discusses repeated sforzando punches accumulating. Nothing in the book addresses what
  happens when a perforation of a given expression track is re-punched while that function is
  already active. Your observation that 63 % of sforzando-on perforations fall while a latch would
  already be set has no counterpart in him.
- He explicitly declines to decode the dynamics. The preamble to the roll-reading rules:

  > In den folgenden Regeln ist zusammengefaßt, was zum Lesen der Rolle notwendig ist. **Nicht
  > berücksichtigt ist die Dynamik, da diese ohne weitere Untersuchungen noch nicht vollständig
  > entschlüsselt werden kann.**
  >
  > — p. 138

  So footnote 108 is offered as a track map, not as a decoding. He gives the functions and stops
  there. That is worth saying in any citation of him: the enumeration is deliberate and precise, the
  silence about behaviour over time is also deliberate.

## 2. The drawn Nuancierungslinien

**He does not discuss them anywhere in the 1996 book.** I take this to be a clean negative, on
three grounds:

1. No index entry for *Nuancierung*, *Nuancierungslinie*, *Nuancierbalg*, or anything similar. The
   N column on p. 327 runs Nachregulierung – Nancarrow – Neue Sachlichkeit – Neuheit –
   Nichtlinearität – Niederschlag – niedrigste sinnvolle Interpretation – nonlegato – Norm –
   Notation – Notenbeispiel – Notenschrift – Notenwert. No entry for the printed scale either.
2. He uses *Nüance / Nüancierung* only in the ordinary musical sense (pp. 28, 37), never as a term
   for a mechanism.
3. He handled late rolls in the tempo chapter — Nr. 3315 (Sándor László, Schubert *Unvollendete*,
   pp. 78–79, six numbers away from your 3309), Nr. 3822 and 3823/3824 (Gieseking), Nr. 4182/4183
   (Serkin), Nr. 4051 and 4121 — and the entries are purely tempo tables, cm values, take-up
   circumferences and hypothesis weightings. He describes no markings, lines or printed scale on any
   of them.

So the second-hand report you are working from **cannot be confirmed or corrected from his
published work**. If he holds that view it will be from the Sydney 2024 talk, whose title is exactly
right for it ("Insights to the Editing Process of the Dynamics and Pedalling on Welte Mignon Piano
Rolls"). I would ask GPRM for the video rather than rely on the report.

Two things in the book do bear on the question indirectly, and both are worth having.

### (a) Welte did draw rolls before punching them — but for a different product

In the section on the late light-music rolls he quotes Hans Haaß, who was Welte's Tonmeister from
1925, on a **Zeichenmaschine** (drawing machine):

> Wie Haaß selbst schreibt, hatte die Firma eine Zeichenmaschine entwickelt, die eine absolute
> Tempofestlegung und eine genaue Bestimmung kleinster Notenwerte gestattete.
>
> — p. 156, citing Hans Haaß, „Über das Wesen mechanischer Klaviermusik", in: *Musikblätter des
> Anbruch* IX, 1927, S. 351–353

and Haaß's own complaints about its precision, which Gottschewski calls rare and valuable criticism
of the Welte technique from an insider:

> Erstens gibt er zu bedenken, daß »beim Ausstanzen der gezeichneten Noten länger oder kürzer
> werden«, eine Tatsache, die wir auch bei den Reproduktionsrollen immer wieder bemerken mußten
> („Stanzungenauigkeit"). Ferner führten schon minimale Witterungseinflüsse zu Papierveränderungen,
> die »nicht geringe Differenzen in der Takteinteilung« bewirkten. Und schließlich – dieses Argument
> läßt uns aufhorchen – »veranlaßt die mit der Zeichnung Hand in Hand gehende Aufwicklung des
> Papieres auf eine durch das Dickerwerden der Rolle bedingte schnellere Papierwegnahme auf dem
> Zeichenbrett. Somit verändert sich allmählich bis zum Schluß die Takteinteilung und damit auch das
> Tempo des Stücks.«
>
> — p. 156

This establishes that at Welte in the 1920s there was a workflow in which a roll was **drawn on a
moving paper on a drawing board and the drawn notes were then punched out**. That is a genuine
precedent for a drawn line on a roll of exactly your period, and it establishes the drawn-then-
punched order for that product. It does not transfer to 3309 without argument: Gottschewski is
describing the mechanically produced light-music rolls (Nr. 5652–5665 and similar, ~1925–26), not
hand-played art rolls, and he does not suggest that hand-played rolls passed over the
Zeichenmaschine. I would treat it as a fact about the shop and not as evidence about 3309.

### (b) His argument that the dynamics must sit ahead of the notes

This is the closest published thing to the "slightly ahead of the punches" claim, and his reason is
mechanical rather than human:

> Für die Dynamik kann das deshalb nicht zutreffen, weil der Aufnahmeapparat frühestens im
> Augenblick des Anschlags die Anschlagsstärke erkennen kann, während der Wiedergabeapparat den
> dynamischen Level schon vor dem Anschlag erreichen muß. Somit träte mindestens eine
> Zeitverschiebung in der Dynamikregelung von Aufnahme- und Wiedergabeapparat auf.
>
> — p. 45

The recording apparatus can register the striking force at the earliest at the moment of the attack;
the reproducing apparatus must have reached the dynamic level *before* the attack. Hence a time
shift between recording and reproduction is unavoidable. The context is an argument that no
automatic compensation could have been built in, so this is offered as a difficulty, not as a
description of editorial practice. But it is his own published reason why the expression information
on a finished roll has to lead the notes it governs. It is a *different* reason from the
hand-nuancer's reaction time, and if you cite the lead you should not attach his name to the
reaction-time explanation without the video to back it.

## 3. The recording and editing process

He confirms Hagmann's central negative and adds detail on the editing, which is his real
contribution here.

### He defers the technology to Hagmann

From the Vorwort:

> Auf eine Einführung in die Geschichte und Technik des Welte-Mignon-Verfahrens wurde, abgesehen von
> den für das unmittelbare Leseverständnis notwendigen Details, verzichtet; der interessierte Leser
> möge die im Literaturverzeichnis genannte Arbeit von Peter Hagmann zu Rate ziehen.
>
> — p. 5

Everything technical in the book is there only because a reader needs it to follow the argument.
That is why the yield on questions 4 and 5 is thin on pneumatics and rich on the time axis.

### No piano recording rolls survive; organ recording rolls do

> Da es keine Klavier-Aufnahmerollen mehr gibt, ist ein weiterer Aufschluß über das Aufnahme- und
> Bearbeitungsverfahren auf direktem Wege kaum noch zu erwarten. Da jedoch zahlreiche Aufnahmerollen
> für die Welte-Philharmonie-Orgel erhalten sind, könnte einiges durch eine Untersuchung dieser
> Rollen herauskommen, die nach wie vor ein Desiderat darstellt.
>
> — p. 41; the footnote points to the Weiss-Stauffacher collection in Seewen near Basel, citing
> Hagmann, p. 80

He immediately qualifies the value of the organ rolls for our purposes:

> Wie jedoch die von mir behandelten Fälle von Manipulation zeigen, sind die Ursachen für
> Veränderungen an Klavieraufnahmen häufig in Problemen mit Pedal oder Dynamik zu suchen, und über
> diese beiden Aspekte können die Orgelaufnahmen natürlicherweise keinen Aufschluß geben.
>
> — p. 41

So he agrees with Hagmann that the procedure is undocumented, and says the one surviving body of
recording rolls cannot answer the dynamics question.

### The expression tracks were edited after the fact

This is the finding in the book that matters most for question 3, and it is easy to miss because it
appears as item 6 in a list of observations from comparing roll copies:

> 6. In den Steuerspuren tauchen gelegentlich systematische Veränderungen auf. Die als Kopiervorlage
>    verwendeten Rollen lassen erkennen, daß es sich dabei um nachträgliche Änderungen, vermutlich
>    aus technischen Gründen, handelt. Da die Steuerspuren für unsere Analysen keine Bedeutung haben,
>    wurde diesem Umstand nicht weiter nachgegangen.
>
> — p. 40

Systematic changes in the control tracks, demonstrably made after the fact, visible on the rolls
used as copy masters — and he did not pursue them because his subject was tempo. That is, as far as I
can see, the seed of the Sydney 2024 talk, thirty years later.

### Other editing findings

- **Master roll and copies.** Copies were struck from a *Kopiervorlage*; the copy masters "genauer"
  than the retail copies (p. 40, item 7). Rolls carry the punching date, the name of the puncher and
  of the controller (p. 38). He expects a "fortschreitender Editionsprozeß der Mutterrolle", i.e.
  the master was progressively edited between punchings, so *Stanzdatum* and *Aufnahmedatum* are
  different things and copies of one recording may differ (p. 38).
- **Correction marks.** The copy-master rolls in the Freiburg Augustinermuseum "tragen teilweise
  zahlreiche Korrekturvermerke"; pencil marks in the margin make it likely errors were found on
  trial playing and corrected then; errors were removed by re-punching or by pasting over (p. 39).
- **The pianists were consulted.** They were invited to assess the rolls (p. 35, citing Hagmann
  p. 248), and one case is documented by an annotation:

  > Auf einer 1922 gestanzten Rolle, die als Kopiervorlage verwendet wurde (Nr. 3832: Walter
  > Gieseking spielt 6 kleine Klavierstücke op. 19 von Schönberg) ist vermerkt »geändert nach
  > Gieseking 29.2.24«.
  >
  > — p. 35, Fn. 42

  A roll punched in 1922, altered in 1924 on the pianist's say-so. Same punching year as 3309, which
  makes it the most directly comparable editorial evidence he offers.
- **Who decided.** For the manipulations he found, he repeatedly leaves the question open between
  pianist, Tonmeister and editor, and says so: "kann jeweils nur vermutet werden" (p. 40). For the
  strongly altered Saint-Saëns roll Nr. 795 he guesses Edwin Welte himself (p. 141). For the late
  light-music rolls he names Hans Haaß, Tonmeister from 1925 (p. 155).
- **Whether takes were spliced is unanswerable.** "Zu den Fragen, die völlig unbeantwortet bleiben
  müssen, gehört, ob gelegentlich »geschnitten« wurde" (p. 40).
- **The recording session.** The pianist played a grand in a room fitted with a few technical
  devices, was not forced to play into a horn, and could address an audience present in the room
  (p. 36). He is sceptical of the surviving photographs: "Mit Sicherheit sind die Photos gestellt,
  wahrscheinlich gegebenenfalls auch retuschiert. Einige der bekannten Photos sind Photomontagen"
  (p. 36, Fn. 44).
- **Welte's editorial policy.** He argues Welte deliberately left small blemishes in, as a quality
  signal against Hupfeld, and cites an audible wrong trill in a Buhlig roll on his own CD as
  evidence (p. 37).

## 4. Where mezzoforte sits

He gives no number and says nothing about the printed scale. But he does state the phenomenon that
your two measurements exhibit, and he names its source.

> **29** Genaugenommen gibt es zwei sehr dicht beieinanderliegende mittlere Positionen, in denen die
> Lautstärke arretiert werden kann, je nachdem, von welcher Seite der Regulierbalg an die
> Arretierungsstelle gebracht wird. (Vgl. Hans-W. Schmitz, *Welte-Mignon und Hupfeld DEA, Zwei
> Reproduktionssysteme in Konkurrenz*, Das Mechanische Musikinstrument VI, 1981, Nr. 19, S. 4.)
>
> — p. 29, Fn. 29 (verified against a high-resolution render)

"Strictly speaking there are two very closely adjacent middle positions at which the loudness can be
arrested, depending on which side the regulating bellows is brought to the arresting point from."

You measure the Mezzoforte hook arresting the bellows at **0.627 and 0.645** of the travel. That is
two closely adjacent positions, and the attribution to the direction of approach is exactly
Schmitz's explanation as Gottschewski reports it. He does not give the values, so this corroborates
the shape of your finding and not its magnitude. The primary source for the number, if one exists,
is Schmitz 1981, not Gottschewski.

Two further points of terminology and structure that support your reading:

- His name for the function is **Mezzofortearretierung**, an *arrest*, and the tracks are
  "Mezzofortearretierung aus / ein" (p. 138, Fn. 108). It arrests the bellows where it catches it. It
  is not a level that is commanded.
- In the main text the mezzoforte is one of three exceptions to continuous variation: "außer bei der
  geringsten, der höchsten und zu Steuerungszwecken einer mittleren Lautstärke" (p. 28). The
  qualifier *zu Steuerungszwecken*, "for control purposes", is his, and reads as a mechanism for
  re-establishing a known state rather than as a musical dynamic level.

### Clean negatives

- **No statement anywhere about the printed P.P. / M.F. / F.F. scale.** No index entry, nothing in
  the text I read.
- **No statement about the linearity of loudness against bellows travel or against vacuum.** He
  discusses linearity at length, but always of *paper speed*, never of dynamics.
- **No number for where mezzoforte sits.** Nothing that could be compared with 0.627 / 0.645 beyond
  "two very closely adjacent middle positions".

So he does not contradict your measurement that mezzoforte sits well above the midpoint the printed
scale implies. He simply never relates the mechanism to the printed scale at all.

on the **time axis**, because that was his subject. On the pneumatics he is nearly as empty as
Hagmann.

### The spool acceleration, and why it matters for dynamics

He rejects a constant 5 cm/s outright:

> Leider hat sich durch die im Folgenden mitgeteilte Untersuchung herausgestellt, daß eine konstante
> Papiergeschwindigkeit von 5 cm/s nicht zu einem authentischen Tempoverhalten führt, sondern daß
> sich die Papiergeschwindigkeit zum Ende der Rolle hin allmählich beschleunigen muß.
>
> — p. 43

and states the consequence for expression coding twice, in terms that are almost a specification for
what you are building:

> Auch für die Berechnung der Dynamiksteuerspuren wäre eine konstante Papiergeschwindigkeit von
> großem Vorteil gewesen, weil die Lautstärkeänderung zwischen zwei Punkten auf der Rolle nicht von
> dem Längenabstand auf dem Papier, sondern von der zwischen diesen Punkten verstreichenden Zeit
> abhängt. Bei veränderlicher Papiergeschwindigkeit müßte für eine korrekte Berechnung der Dynamik
> also die jeweils aktuelle Papiergeschwindigkeit berücksichtigt werden.
>
> — p. 47

> Bei sehr langen Rollen übersteigt die Beschleunigung des Papiers bei konstanter
> Walzengeschwindigkeit 30%, so daß eine Berücksichtigung dieser Beschleunigung für die Berechnung
> der Dynamik unbedingt nötig wäre.
>
> — p. 48

The change in loudness between two points depends on the **elapsed time**, not on the **distance on
the paper**. Over a very long roll the paper accelerates by more than 30 % at constant spool
angular velocity, and he calls accounting for it "unbedingt nötig" for computing the dynamics. If
your emulator integrates against pixel rows rather than against seconds, this is the correction.

Further figures on the same problem:

- Windmotor slows by **more than 10 %** on long rolls (p. 46).
- Constant paper speed is achievable up to a roll length of about **8 minutes** (p. 46, Fn. 69,
  from Schmitz's experiments).
- Best empirical fit: linear paper-speed development with **~80 % effect of the circumference
  increase** (p. 134). He nevertheless adopts the constant-spool hypothesis throughout the book.
- End tempo of very long rolls constrained to **±5 %** (p. 51).
- Humidity: winding volume varied ~**9 %** between measurements before and after the start of the
  heating season, worth at most **2 %** on the final circumference (p. 48, Fn. 72).
- Uneven winding produces wow of up to **5 %** per take-up revolution in extreme cases (p. 48,
  Fn. 72).
- Roll length between copies varies by **0.5 %** (p. 39); overall tempo effect ~0.5 % (p. 40).
- Group means of the "plausibelste Interpretation" by recording date (p. 133, Tabelle 6): 1905 group
  84.9 %, 1908–1913 group 71.5 %, the late group (Nr. 3315 and 4182/3) 91.3 %. One-way ANOVA finds no
  significant difference (F = 0.13 against a 75 %-level threshold of 1.48), so **no evidence that
  the speed behaviour depends on recording date**.

### The law itself, and what the emulator now runs on

Chapter V (pp. 135–137) derives the conversion from centimetres on the roll to seconds, and rule 2
of the *Rollenleseregeln* (p. 139) restates it as a working recipe. The derivation is short. Under
the *Walzengeschwindigkeitshypothese* the take-up spool turns at a constant rate, so the paper speed
follows the spool's circumference. The layers lie approximately equally thick, so one centimetre of
paper adds `d · 1 cm²` to the spool's cross-section Q, and with Q = U²/4π

```
Q(x) = Q₀ + d·x                       Q₀ = U₀²/4π
v(x) = v₀ · √( (Q₀ + d·x) / Q₀ )                                    (p. 135)
t(x) = √(Q₀/d) · (2/v₀) · [ √(Q₀/d + x) − √(Q₀/d) ]                 (p. 136)
```

with x the paper run in cm from a point of known circumference U₀. His constants:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| `d` | 0.0075 cm | layer thickness, from a measured 0.047 cm of circumference per revolution (p. 135) |
| `U₀` | 22.25 cm | standard initial circumference; 22.25–22.35 in most cases, varying with the leader (p. 137, Fn. 107; p. 139) |
| `v₀` | U₀ / 4.64 cm s⁻¹ | 4.64 s per revolution, set so that the first 1.45 m run at Welte's marked 145 cm/30 s (p. 137) |

He also gives the way to run the law at less than full effect (p. 137): falsify the layer thickness,
0.0060 in place of 0.0075 for "80 % Auswirkung der Umfangsvergrößerung". His own tempo comparison
prefers about 80 %, and he adopts 100 % throughout the book, because the 20 % difference is worth
under 5 % of tempo after ten minutes and could as well be the players' own drift (p. 134).

`src/roll/spool.ts` is this law, with `circumferenceEffect` for his 80 % trick, and it is what the
emulator's time axis is built from. The closed form is evaluated per pixel row rather than resampled
into a tempo map: a map written one segment per foot, as SUPRA's is, lags the curve by up to 2.3 ms
mid-segment, which is more than the 1.7 ms a row lasts.

**Against the SUPRA tempo map**, on roll 3309 (16.63 m of music between the first and last hole):

| axis | duration | end speed / start speed |
|---|---|---|
| SUPRA tempo map, 0.22 % per foot compounded | 325.4 s | 1.124 |
| Gottschewski, 100 % effect | 323.1 s | 1.147 |
| Gottschewski, 80 % effect | 327.3 s | 1.120 |
| constant 9.5 ft/min, no acceleration | 344.7 s | 1 |

The two accelerations are close, and SUPRA's falls between his 80 % and his 100 %. His start speed,
4.795 cm/s, is 0.6 % below the 9.5 ft/min the scan declares, which is the difference between a speed
measured as a mean over the first 1.45 m and the same figure read as an instantaneous one.

**What the choice is worth to the fit: almost nothing, and the roll cannot decide it.** The whole
roll under the fitted constants, which were fitted on the scan map and so hold the advantage
(`node src/cli/evaluate.ts --fit docs/fit-pneumatic.json [--timing scan] [--effect] [--revolution]`).
The durations are over the traced span, which begins 11 cm before the first hole and so runs a
little longer than the musical length above:

| axis | duration | bass RMSE | treble RMSE |
|---|---|---|---|
| SUPRA tempo map | 328.8 s | 0.03182 | 0.04336 |
| spool, 100 % effect, 4.64 s per revolution | 326.4 s | 0.03199 | 0.04354 |
| spool, 100 % effect, held to the map's duration | 328.8 s | 0.03189 | 0.04339 |
| spool, 80 % effect, held to the same | 328.8 s | 0.03180 | 0.04336 |
| spool, 50 % effect, held to the same | 328.8 s | 0.03174 | 0.04339 |
| spool, 0 % effect, held to the same | 328.8 s | 0.03191 | 0.04363 |

Everything from full spool acceleration to none at all lies within 0.0003, on a fit whose own
residual is 0.032 and whose ablation treats 0.0028 as the noise between refits. The order is not
even stable: 50 % effect scores marginally best in the Bass and 80 % in the Discant, which is what a
quantity the data cannot see looks like. The duration has to be separated from the shape before any
of this can be read, since either alone shifts every rate the fit holds, and `alternatingBlocks`
cuts the roll in seconds, so a changed axis moves the train/test split as well as the model.

So the drawn line is no witness on the acceleration, and the choice rests on provenance.
Gottschewski's constants are measured and stated; SUPRA's 0.22 % per foot has none in either
upstream repository (`docs/prior-art.md` §F). That is why the emulator now runs on his, and the
scores above played no part in it.

Two caveats worth keeping in view. His U₀ is the circumference where the measurement starts, and it
varies with the leader already wound on; roll 3309's leader is about 1.14 m, longer than the ones
that gave him 22.25–22.35 cm, so 22.25 may sit slightly low here. And `out/{druid}/curves.csv` still
carries a `seconds` column from the SUPRA map, which is what the Python in `analysis/` measures
against; the emulator does not read that column, but figures in `docs/empirics.md` are on the older
axis to within the 1.6 % that separates the two locally.

### On dynamic differentiation by displacement

> Kritisch wird die Differenzierung bei Tonabständen unter ca. 3 mm, da man wegen der
> Stanzungenauigkeit die Reproduktion der Betonung auf den Rollenkopien unsicher wird. Bei exakter
> Stanzung reichen schon 2 mm (ca. 0,04 s) Abstand zwischen den Tönen für eine deutlich hörbare
> Differenzierung aus.
>
> — p. 147

- Deliberate asynchrony introduced by editors: **3–5 mm** typically, 2 mm at the margin of
  reliability, and in the Edwin Fischer example a regular **ca. 2 mm (1–2.5 mm)** (pp. 148–149).
- "Real" arpeggios often exceed **5 mm** (p. 149).
- Bass notes deliberately lengthened by **~1 cm ≈ 0.2 s**, sometimes several cm (p. 142).
- More than **85 %** of tracker holes are covered by paper in normal playing (p. 45).
- **pp notes are delayed more than ff notes** on reproduction; whether this was compensated at
  punching is unknown (p. 139, rule 4; p. 28, Fn. 27).

That last pair is a useful bound on the rate you are fitting. If 2 mm ≈ 40 ms of offset yields a
clearly audible dynamic difference within one keyboard half, then the fast crescendo traverses an
audible interval in something like 40 ms.

### What he does not give

- **No vacuum figure at all.** He uses *Saugwindspannung*, *Saugspannung* and *Vorpneumatik* as
  terms (pp. 28, 43, 44, index p. 329) and never attaches a number to any of them. On this he adds
  nothing to Hagmann.
- **No time constant for the bellows.** The nearest is the qualitative "weil eine gewisse Zeit
  benötigt wird, um vom *ff* bis zum *pp* oder umgekehrt zu gelangen" (p. 28), with no figure.
- **No crescendo duration.** He never says how long a crescendo takes.
- **No figure for the mezzoforte position** (see §4).

## 6. Other things that change how the punched code should be read

### The full track map

Worth having in full because it is more explicit than most published layouts, and because it
supersedes his own looser description on p. 28:

> Die Spurbelegung für das 100er-System (vgl. S. 26) lautet (von links nach rechts):
>
> Spur 1–6: Dynamik für die Töne C₁ bis fis¹
> Spur 7–8: linkes Pedal aus/ein
> Spur 9–10: Widerstand aus/ein
> Spur 11–90: Töne C₁–g⁴
> Spur 91: Rücklauf
> Spur 92: nicht belegt
> Spur 93–94: rechtes Pedal ein/aus
> Spur 95–100: Dynamik für die Töne g¹–g⁴
>
> — p. 138

With Fn. 108, from the outside inwards on each side: MF-arrest off, MF-arrest on, slow crescendo off
(= slow decrescendo), slow crescendo on, sudden decrescendo, sudden crescendo. So on the bass side
track 1 is MF-off and track 6 is sudden crescendo; on the treble side track 100 is MF-off and track
95 is sudden crescendo. Note that tracks 9–10 are the *Widerstand* for the wind motor, an electrical
resistance that only reduces motor noise in low-demand passages and "hat keine störenden Auswirkungen
auf das System" (p. 28, Fn. 26).

### The 98-note system

> Später wurde von der Firma Welte ein abweichendes System mit 98 Lochspuren eingeführt, das (unter
> Verlust der Dynamik) mit dem amerikanischen Standard kompatibel war. […] Nach Einführung dieses
> neuen Systems wurden alle Rollen (auch die älteren) für beide Systeme gestanzt und verkauft. Es
> wäre wünschenswert, daß jemand sich die Mühe unterzieht, die Umstanzungen der alten Rollen zu
> vergleichen, um die Feststellung von Alphons Eschles zu überprüfen, daß die späteren Rollen
> »schlechte Kopien« seien.
>
> — p. 26, Fn. 23

Relevant if any 3309 material you hold could be a 98-note re-punch rather than an original T-100
punching. He flags the comparison as an open task; the Prelude 11 paper above suggests he has since
taken it up.

### Deliberate displacement of perforations

Section A-3 I.4, *Ungleichzeitigkeit zur dynamischen Differenzierung* (pp. 147–149), is the fullest
published treatment I know of. His conclusions:

- Because dynamics are controlled only per keyboard half, differentiating two near-simultaneous
  notes **within one half** requires displacing one of them in time.
- The possibility was **often not used**: many chords are punched synchronously and simply do not
  bring the melody out. So the presence of an accent in the music is not evidence that the editor
  encoded one.
- Where it was used, the displacement is 3–5 mm and, in his Edwin Fischer example, a systematic ~2 mm
  delay of the melody note relative to the chord.
- Whether to use it "lag […] bis zu einem hohen Grade im Ermessen des betreuenden Tonmeisters"
  (p. 149).

For a nuancing-line model this matters as a caution: some of what looks like accent shaping in the
note track is editorial displacement rather than anything the expression tracks did.

### Octave additions to get past the ff ceiling

> Da alle Oktavverdreifachungen an *ff*-Stellen oder bei kräftigen Akzenten auftreten, kann vermutet
> werden, daß auch hier ein Mangel der Wiedergabe kompensiert werden sollte: die obere Grenze für den
> *ff*-Anschlag.
>
> — p. 151

Octaves were added at *ff* passages and strong accents to compensate a ceiling the mechanism could
not exceed. He notes these cases cluster in **very early rolls (recording date 1905 or earlier)** and
then again in the **late light-music rolls**, and explicitly says this date pattern does not hold for
the other kinds of manipulation (p. 151). If your fit shows the bellows saturating at the top, this
is independent evidence that the ceiling was a felt limitation.

### Late long rolls may be meant to play slower

> Gelegentlich gibt es Rollen mit dem Hinweis »Tempo langsamer stellen«. […] Alle mir bekannt
> gewordenen Rollen mit diesem Hinweis stammen aus einer späten Produktionsperiode. […] auch bei
> anderen Rollen mit langer Spieldauer aus einer späten Produktionsperiode (nach dem ersten
> Weltkrieg), die diesen Hinweis nicht tragen, muß mindestens die Möglichkeit in Betracht gezogen
> werden, daß sie langsamer abgespielt werden sollen.
>
> — p. 43, Fn. 59

3309 is post-WWI. If it is long, the intended playing speed may be below nominal, which would scale
every rate you fit.

### Where he differs from Hagmann

I found **no place where Gottschewski corrects Hagmann**. He defers to him for the technology
(p. 5), cites him approvingly throughout, and adopts his account of reproduction aesthetics
(pp. 32, 158). The one substantive divergence is the sforzando, and Gottschewski does not present it
as a disagreement — he simply enumerates the six tracks in footnote 108 in a way that does not
admit Hagmann's latch. Since he never engages Hagmann on the point, the footnote is best cited as
independent testimony rather than as a refutation.

His authority for the dynamics mechanism, where he has one, is **Hans-W. Schmitz** rather than
Hagmann. He points to Schmitz for "die genaue Funktion der Dynamik-Steuerspuren" (p. 28, Fn. 25) and
for the two mezzoforte arrest positions (p. 29, Fn. 29), both to:

> Hans-W. Schmitz, *Welte-Mignon und Hupfeld DEA, Zwei Reproduktionssysteme in Konkurrenz*, Das
> Mechanische Musikinstrument VI, 1981, Nr. 19, S. 3–10.

and to the firm's own brochure, *Das Welte-Mignon Reproduktions-Klavier*, o. O. u. J., of which he
cites pp. 20–21 for the wind-motor regulation. Schmitz 1981 is where the quantitative answers to
question 4 would be, if they are anywhere.

## Summary of clean negatives

Things he simply does not address, all of them useful as such:

1. The word *Sforzando*, and the latch question as a question.
2. Accumulation of repeated expression punches.
3. The drawn Nuancierungslinien on late red rolls.
4. The printed P.P. / M.F. / F.F. scale.
5. Any numerical value for the mezzoforte position.
6. Any vacuum figure.
7. Any time constant, rate, or crescendo duration for the bellows.
8. The linearity of loudness against bellows travel.

And one explicit self-limitation, which is the honest frame for all of the above: "Nicht
berücksichtigt ist die Dynamik, da diese ohne weitere Untersuchungen noch nicht vollständig
entschlüsselt werden kann" (p. 138).
