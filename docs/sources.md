# What the sources say about the T-100 expression mechanism

Everything the literature, the patents, the regulation instructions, the prior software and
Gottschewski's Sydney talk say that bears on emulating the Nuancierbälge and pedals of a red
Welte roll. Arranged by topic. Each claim carries its source. A statement without one is this
repository's reading and is marked as such. The measurements made on roll 3309 are in
`measurements.md`, the fitted constants in `fit-pneumatic.json` and `experiments.md`.

## 1. The roll and its code

**Track map.** Anhang 10, confirmed by Gottschewski 1996 p. 138 with n. 108 and by Peetz 2004
p. 10. The MIDI keys are those of Stanford's roll-image-parser (key = hole + 13).

| Hole | Function | MIDI | Hole | Function | MIDI |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| 1 | bass Mezzoforte off | 14 | 100 | treble Mezzoforte off | 113 |
| 2 | bass Mezzoforte on | 15 | 99 | treble Mezzoforte on | 112 |
| 3 | bass Crescendo off | 16 | 98 | treble Crescendo off | 111 |
| 4 | bass Crescendo on | 17 | 97 | treble Crescendo on | 110 |
| 5 | bass Sforzando off | 18 | 96 | treble Sforzando off | 109 |
| 6 | bass Sforzando on | 19 | 95 | treble Sforzando on | 108 |
| 7 | Hammerleiste off | 20 | 94 | Pedal off | 107 |
| 8 | Hammerleiste on | 21 | 93 | Pedal on | 106 |
| 9 | Widerstand off | 22 | 92 | unused | 105 |
| 10 | Widerstand on | 23 | 91 | rewind | 104 |
| 11–90 | notes C₁–g⁴ | 24–103 | | | |

The two sides mirror one another, and on each side the cancel line lies outboard of its set
line, so that a lateral drift of the paper cancels a function rather than triggering one
(Hagmann pp. 97–98 with n. 27). Bass is C₁–f♯¹, treble g¹–g⁴ (Hagmann p. 96). midi2exp's
`setupRedWelte` puts the soft pedal on holes 9/10 against its own comment and every other source.
The emulator follows Anhang 10 (`src/roll/expression.ts`).

**The Widerstand** (holes 9/10) switches the blower motor between its two speeds and so changes
the vacuum available both to the notes and to the relay (Hagmann pp. 73–75, p. 98 n. 29).
Gottschewski takes it for a device that only reduces motor noise and „hat keine störenden
Auswirkungen auf das System" (1996, p. 28 n. 26). midi2exp and pianolatron ignore it. The
emulator carries it as a term (`windRateGain`, `windTargetShift`), and the ablation finds it worth
nothing measurable on roll 3309 (`experiments.md`).

**Dimensions.** The paper is 328 mm wide. Perforations are 2.2 mm in diameter until about 1910
and 1.8 mm after, on a 0.9 mm step, with tracks 3.2 mm apart (Hagmann p. 76). Stahnke 1997
measures the bar at 12.902 in = 327.71 mm with ports 0.12526 in = 3.1816 mm apart, derived from
the foot of Lahr, and notes 102 punch positions across the paper of which a 100-port bar reads
100. A single roll gave a hole diameter of 0.078 in ≈ 1.98 mm (Bob Billings reporting Stahnke,
MMD 8 February 2003). The glide-block openings are rectangular (Hagmann p. 97, Peetz 2004 p. 9).
The tracker bore the emulator uses is midi2exp's 1.413 mm (`Expressionizer.h:120`). Gottschewski
gives the hole length as 1.3 mm (1996, p. 139 rule 5). Whether expression and note holes share a
diameter is stated nowhere.

**On and off.** On the T-100 a function commanded by an on/off pair holds until its cancel line
is read. On the T-98 „die Funktion bleibt genau so lange ausgeführt, als die entsprechende
Perforation im Notenband über die Gleitblock-Oeffnung läuft" (Hagmann p. 101). Hagmann draws this
distinction deliberately in both chapters, and reading the T-98 rule onto a T-100 roll would turn
every short trigger punch into a short function. An off command acts only when the perforation has
left the tracker hole, so it acts about 1.3 mm ≈ 26 ms later than an on command at the same edge.
Gottschewski's rule 5 corrects this by lengthening every perforation by 1.3 mm at its tail before
converting to time (1996, p. 139). Pfeffer, ch. 1, P4 states the same rule with reference to
Phillips, *Piano Rolls and Contemporary Player Pianos*, p. 178. midi2exp and pianolatron do the
same in ticks (§9). The shortest impulse the system can carry is 1.3 + 1.5 mm ≈ 56 ms (Gottschewski
1996, p. 30 n. 32).

**Time axis.** Welte marks the Skalarollen at 143.1 to 150 cm per 30 s, centred near 145 cm
(Gottschewski 1996, p. 42, and Pfeffer, ch. 1, n. 182 gives the same range). Welte recommends
„Tempo 70" for the accents to come out right (Hagmann p. 85 quoting Welte 13/17), which the
scale roll calibrates so that its two control tones lie half a minute apart (Hagmann p. 84,
control 1). A Welte drives the take-up spool at a constant rate of revolution, so the paper
accelerates as the spool fills. Gottschewski derives the law (1996, pp. 135–137, rule 2 p. 139):
a wound layer of 0.0075 cm adds 0.047 cm of circumference per turn, the cross-section grows
linearly with paper run, so speed grows as its square root. His constants are a standard initial
circumference of 22.25 cm (22.25–22.35 in most cases, depending on the leader) and one revolution
in 4.64 s, set so that the first 1.45 m take 30 s. His tempo comparison prefers about 80 % of the
geometric effect, which he obtains by using 0.0060 for the layer thickness, and he adopts 100 % in
the book (p. 134, p. 137). He rejects a constant 5 cm/s (p. 43), notes that the acceleration
exceeds 30 % on very long rolls (p. 48), that the wind motor itself slows by more than 10 % on long
rolls (p. 46), that Schmitz's experiments found constant speed achievable for about eight minutes
(p. 46 n. 69), and that dynamics depend on elapsed time rather than on paper distance, so a
dynamics calculation must use the current paper speed (p. 47). `src/roll/spool.ts` is this law
with his constants.

Other accelerations in use. SUPRA's raw MIDI carries a tempo map that compounds the speed by
1.0022 per foot (measured on roll 3309: 54 events one foot apart, ratio constant to seven digits),
which is the pre-2021 midi2exp form of 0.22 % per foot. midi2exp and pianolatron now use a linear
0.3147 ft/min² without a stated source. Bärtsch proposes 0.015 mm/s², and Bern calculates the
acceleration so that the correction over ten minutes is 1 % of the length (Pfeffer, ch. 1, n. 183,
with the attribution of the 0.22 % to Stahnke through a comment in roll-image-parser). On roll 3309
the SUPRA map and Gottschewski's law differ by 0.7 % in duration and by at most 1.6 % in any one
step, and the drawn line cannot tell them apart (`measurements.md` §12). The emulator uses
Gottschewski's because his constants are measured and stated.

Two cautions on the axis. Paper transport is not uniform beyond the spool effect, since the wind
motor's regulating valve „vermag die Stockungen im Transport des Notenbandes nicht vollständig zu
vermeiden" (Hagmann p. 78). And some late long rolls are marked „Tempo langsamer stellen", so for
other long post-war rolls the possibility that they were meant to play slower should be kept in
view (Gottschewski 1996, p. 43 n. 59). Roll 3309 is post-war and 16.6 m long.

## 2. The relay

Each of the six functions has a relay unit of the same anatomy (Hagmann p. 98, described for
the crescendo and said to hold for the others): glide-block opening → conduit → membrane
chamber → membrane → valve, with a bleed bore from the chamber back to the common vacuum chamber
15. Chamber 15 is fed „in der jeweils vom Gebläse erzeugten Stärke" (p. 98), and on Anhang 13
conduit 14 runs unbranched from chamber 15 to the node where the trunk leaves the magazine
bellows, upstream of the cone valve. US 1,008,291 states the same, the expression actions being
„controlled directly by said main bellows instead of by the regulating bellows". So the relay
runs on the supply vacuum, which the Regelbalg does not regulate, and every closing rate of the
bellows scales with it.

| Function | Opening | Conduit | Chamber / membrane / bleed | Valve | To the Nuancierung | Hold chamber |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Sforzando on | 12 | 18 | 19 / 21 / 20 | 22 | 23, wide | 24/25 on the drawing, see §4 |
| Sforzando off | 11 | 27 | 28 / 30 / 29 | 31 | 33, to auxiliary bellows 94 and 95, and closes valve 22 | none |
| Crescendo on | 10 | 34 | 35 / 36 / 37 | 38 | 39, „mittelbreit" | 40/41 |
| Crescendo off | 9 | 43 | 44 / 45 / 46 | 47 | 48, releases the hold | none |
| Mezzoforte on | 8 | – | 50 / 51 / 52 | 53 | 54, to Mezzofortebalg 93 | 55/56 |
| Mezzoforte off | 7 | – | 59 / 60 / 61 | – | 62, releases the hold | none |

Hagmann names the crescendo parts and 40, 41, 48. The rest are read off Anhang 13 and its
bottom label row, with bores 20 and 29 confirmed by name in controls 4b and 4e. The T-98 relay on
Anhang 14 has no hold chamber on any unit.

**The valve fires on a puff and has a duration threshold.** The bleed bore is dimensioned so
that it restores the vacuum in the chamber „nicht aber den eintretenden Luftstoss zu
neutralisieren vermag" (p. 98). The chamber is a leaky integrator and the membrane lifts on the
pulse, and Welte norms the threshold. Control 4b: six short Sforzando-on perforations must move the
bellows „in sechs Schritten von der Piano- zur Mezzoforte-Stellung", after it has fallen back six
still shorter ones „dürfen sich nicht bewegen", and if mezzoforte is reached too early or too late
bore 20 is adjusted (p. 182). The emulator's `tripThreshold` and `membraneFillMs` are this.

**The valve releases with a tail.** The crescendo-off valve „senkt sich in jenem Masse, als die
Membrankammer 44 über die Bohrung 46 evakuiert wird" (p. 99), so it passes through partial lifts
over a time set by the bleed. Control 4e norms that tail for the sforzando-off unit at bore 29:
three short single cancelling perforations must return the bellows to piano „in drei Schritten"
(p. 183). This is `valveTailMs`. It is also the reason the two valves have separate fill constants
(`membraneFillMs`, `assistFillMs`), since Welte adjusts them at separate bores.

**The latch.** Crescendo-on lifts membranes 36 and 40, rigidly wired together. The valve
chamber's vacuum also reaches the upper half of chamber 41, so membrane 40 stays up by pressure
difference and the function survives the end of the perforation „bis die Funktion über die
Oeffnung 9 des Gleitblocks ausgelöst wird" (pp. 98–99). Cancelling admits vacuum to the lower
half through conduit 48. Mezzoforte works „in gleicher Weise" (p. 100). Brougher adds that the
lock valves bleed to atmosphere under felt covers (2011). DRP 162708 already has three membrane
chambers over six relays: „Dadurch ist erreicht, daß man im Notenblatt nur kurze Öffnungen
vorzusehen braucht, um lang anhaltende Wirkungen hervorzubringen."

**Supply, sag, ceiling.** The blower runs at constant output into a spring-loaded magazine
bellows that buffers demand „kurzfristig" and a safety valve that clamps the vacuum from above.
Spring force and safety-valve setting „bestimmen die Stärke des Fortissimo-Anschlags" (Hagmann
p. 73). Under full chords the trunk vacuum sags (p. 74, said for the T-98 equalising valve, which
exists to detect it), and on the T-100 the roll answers with the Widerstand. No reservoir volume,
spring rate, response time or vacuum figure is given anywhere. The one vacuum figure in the book,
28 mbar (p. 72), is the organ's magazine bellows. Brougher's restorer targets are 5 in WC for the
first intensity and 35 or 32 in WC maximum, with mezzoforte at 0.6 of the maximum and the hook
adding no more than 2 in (2011). The emulator's `supplyDroop` tests a sag with note density, and
roll 3309 shows none (`measurements.md` §9).

## 3. The Nuancierbalg and its paths

One Nuancierbalg per half, spring-opened and suction-closed (Hagmann p. 96). Fully open is
pianissimo and fully closed fortissimo (Pfeffer, ch. 1, P1). It pulls the cone valve 87 off its
seat against spring 89 through a chain band over a roller (p. 96). Between piano, mezzoforte and
forte the bellows „befindet sich in steter Bewegung" and cannot be held exactly (pp. 96–97).

| Path | Through | Far end | Width | Adjuster, control |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| crescendo close | conduit 39 | vacuum, chamber 15 via valve 38 | „mittelbreit" (p. 98) | Drossel 98, control 3 |
| crescendo open (decrescendo) | conduit 39 with bore 100 | atmosphere, chamber 17 via valve 38 | 39 alone admits too little air and 100 makes up the deficit (p. 99) | Drossel 100, control 3 |
| sforzando close | conduit 23 | vacuum via valve 22 | „im Vergleich zur Kondukte 39 breiter dimensioniert" (p. 99) | Drossel 99, control 4a (p. 102 n. 40) |
| sforzando open | throttle 96, opened by auxiliary bellows 94 | atmosphere | set by 96 | Drossel 96, controls 4a, 4c, 4d, 2b |

Conduit 39 is joined to the bellows permanently and the crescendo relay only switches its far
end, so the slow decrescendo is the state of that conduit whenever the crescendo is cancelled.
Schmitz describes the same thing from the other side: two suction inlets, the larger for
forzando-forte and the smaller for crescendo-forte, adjustable at D 1 and D 2, and a permanent
bleed D 4 through which the bellows „wird sich … langsam selbsttätig öffnen" whenever neither is
closing it, so that there is no valve for the slow decrescendo at all (1981, pp. 3–4). The
Skala-Rolle 98 warns that adjusting „Crescendo-P" changes „Crescendo-F", which must then be
rechecked (§3), since the bleed and the suction path share one bellows. This is why midi2exp's
separate rule that a slow decay always runs is a consequence here rather than a rule, and why a
sforzando runs faster with the crescendo latched than without (`measurements.md` §4).

**Symmetry is a regulation target.** Bore 100 exists so that opening takes „derselben zeitlichen
Ausdehnung" as closing (p. 99). Throttle 96 is set so that the reopening after a sforzando takes
the same time as the closing (p. 100). Control 4a demands that the four movements piano to
mezzoforte and back happen „in gleicher Geschwindigkeit" (p. 182), and control 6c that nine
Sforzando on-off pairs hold the bellows at mezzoforte (p. 184). The ablation variants „crescendo
pair symmetric" and „sforzando pair symmetric" test these ties. Roll 3309 satisfies the first
and contradicts the second, the fall being about four times the rise (`measurements.md` §4).

**The sforzando cancel is momentary and cumulative.** Valve 31 lifts while opening 11 is
covered, plus the tail at bore 29. Conduit 33 evacuates bellows 94 and 95, and when the
perforation ends the auxiliaries refill „sogleich" (p. 103, said of the common nuancing unit) and
both throttles close. So closing valve 22 cancels the sforzando permanently while the reopening
assist through 96 acts only for the length of the cancelling perforation: a long cancel returns
the bellows fully (control 4c), a short one „nicht vollständig" (4d), three short ones in three
steps (4e). The emulator integrates the cancel over its perforation (`sforzandoAssistRate`,
`assistFillMs`).

**A fifth path bypasses the bellows.** Bellows 95 opens throttle 97, which admits air „in den
linken Teil der Windkammer 86 und die Kondukte 88", the delivery side downstream of the cone
valve, for „den sofortigen Abbau des Unterdrucks" at a subito piano (p. 100). Controls 2b
(mezzoforte to piano, corrected at „96, eventuell 97") and 5 (forte to piano, at 97) regulate it
apart from the bellows. Schmitz has the same two forzando-piano bellows, one venting the
Nuancierbalg, whose „Spreizfeder" then opens it, the other admitting air into the delivery box
(1981, p. 3), and the Skala-Rolle 98 regulates the second by the hoses of its „Lufteinlaßbälgchen"
(§5). A trace of bellows position cannot show it. A trace of delivered vacuum would show a
downward spike at every cancel. Not modelled, by design.

**The crescendo does not reach fortissimo.** A crescendo alone reaches a forte „bei dem sich der
Balg zwischen mittlerer und vollständig geschlossener Stellung befindet", and only the sforzando
governs the range above (Pfeffer, ch. 1, P3, P4). Gottschewski's slide 14 says a long crescendo
forte „will converge to a certain level ('WELTE LOUD')", which „can be adjusted, but is not
specified in any technical manual". midi2exp caps this at `welte_loud`. The emulator has it as the
asymptote `crescendoTarget`, which the fit puts short of the closed rail in both halves
(`fit-pneumatic.json`); the phase-plane measurement gives 0.77 in the bass and cannot pin it down in
the treble (`measurements.md` §3).

**The curves are not linear.** Schmitz draws the basic movements as straight lines and says at
once that „Tatsächlich erreichen die Abläufe nur annähernd eine Geradenform", naming the bellows
spring, the pull of the band on the roller and the non-linear loss of volume of a closing wedge as
causes (1981, p. 4). His Bild 4 draws the four movements against a one-second bar, derived from
rolls with lines: forzando forte about 0.2 s from pp to ff and nearly straight, forzando piano
about 0.15 s, crescendo forte an S that reaches mezzoforte in 1.3 to 1.4 s and 0.9 of the span at
2.4 s, flattening above 0.8 without reaching ff, crescendo piano a decay that a single exponential
of τ ≈ 0.70 s fits to 0.03 of the span (digitised from the 300 dpi scan, the drawing being the
larger uncertainty).
Gottschewski: „The crescendos and diminuendos of Welte instruments are by no way linear
processes. For example, the curves of the crescendo piano, which means slow diminuendo, look like
an exponential decay", so the time from fortissimo to mezzoforte is much shorter than from
mezzoforte to pianissimo, and „errors can add up over time" in a linear emulation (2024, 09:25,
10:04). Pfeffer, ch. 1, n. 51 attributes the observation to him. The emulator's flow law
`dx/dt = g·a·sign(T − x)·|T − x|^α` contains the constant rate at α = 0, the orifice at α = ½ and
the laminar exponential at α = 1, and fits α.

**The 1904 design.** DRP 162708 describes the ancestor: relay r¹ forte with a direct tube *l*
to the control bellows *e*, r² piano with „ein engeres Rohr *m*" to a small valve bellows *n*
that lifts a valve off vent *o* in the bellows wall, r³ crescendo through „ein ganz enges Rohr
*p*", r⁴ its release, r⁵ and r⁶ for the mezzoforte bellows *h*. Rate is set by tube bore, and the
piano vent is the ancestor of bellows 94 and throttle 96. A cord from the slide *c* runs over
roller *g* on the control bellows to the regulating bellows *d*. Kaiser 2002 reports Lightcap
that this became „a ladder chain over a sprocket opening a cone-shaped valve" around 1922, a
single source for the date. The chain-and-cone form itself is drawn by Hagmann (Anhang 13, 14), by
Schmitz (1981, Bild 1: „flexibles Band" over the „Rollenachse R" on the Nuancierbalg to the
„Kegelventil K") and in the factory cross-section of the Skala-Rolle 98 (p. 15, band B over a
roller on the Nuancierbalg N to the cone K, leather nut M on the Regelbalg R).

**Bore ratios cannot be read off the sketches.** Anhang 13 draws conduits 23, 33, 39 and 54 as
single pen strokes 3 to 6 px wide on a 1744 × 2480 px bilevel scan of the A4 sheet, with no
difference between 23 and 39 although Hagmann states one in words, and draws the throttles as
adjusting screws without seat diameters. Any ratio taken from the plate would be invented. The
only relative information is verbal: 23 wider than 39, 39 medium, 100 a supplement too small to
replace 39. Every conductance has to be fitted.

## 4. Does the sforzando latch?

The sources split, and they split by type.

For a latch: Hagmann, p. 99, on valve 22, „Und auch hier wird die Funktion aufrecht erhalten,
selbst wenn die entsprechende Perforation verschwindet", said by analogy with the crescendo („In
gleicher Weise arbeitet das (einfache) Ventil 22"), the latch mechanism having been described
once, for the crescendo. Anhang 13 draws a hold chamber 24/25 with cancel conduit 32 on the
Sforzando-on unit, but the three „on" units there are one hand-drawn module repeated at a pitch
of 331 px, the plate does not distinguish the „single" valve 22 from the „double" valve 38, and
the plate is after a T-98 manual. DRP 162708 says of the forte relay that once its hole has
passed, the control bellows „durch das Crescendorelais in Fortestellung gehalten wird", which is
a hold, though through the crescendo rather than through a forte latch.

Against a latch: control 4b (six short pulses give six steps and stop at mezzoforte, with bore
20 trimming how much travel a pulse buys), 6a (five Sforzando on-off pairs during a crescendo must
not stop the bellows closing fully), 6b (after five short Sforzando-on perforations „trotz des
automatischen Eintritts der Decrescendo-Bewegung" the bellows must reach forte each time) and 6c
(nine on-off pairs hold mezzoforte), all in Anhang 12, pp. 182–184, and all T-100 controls.
Gottschewski 1996, p. 138 n. 108: the last track pair „handelt es sich um zwei verschiedene
Funktionen, die auch gleichzeitig wirken können", in contrast to the two pairs that switch one
function off and on, which is why the T-98 could reduce the dynamics to four tracks. Gottschewski
2024, slide 26: „forzando forte (a quick crescendo that lasts as long as the hole in the sliding
block is open)". Schmitz, on the T-100's punching: all loudness changes are commanded by holes „die
an einem beliebigen Punkt der Notenrolle ansetzen und die je nach Erfordernis beliebig lang sind",
the smallest forzando-forte hole switches „um etwa 1/6 des gesamten Lautstärkespektrums", a
forzando-piano hole „je nach Ausgangslage um etwa 1/3 bis 1/2", and „jede forzando-Funktion [kann]
zu einem beliebigen Zeitpunkt beendet werden, beispielsweise einer forzando-forte-Lochung von
1,8 mm Länge kann 0,7 mm hinter dem Lochanfang eine forzando-piano-Lochung folgen" (1981, pp. 5–7,
the hole dimensions there being for green rolls). Pfeffer, ch. 1, P4: „wirken Sforzando an und
Sforzando ab nur über die Dauer der eigentlichen Stanzung". Brougher speaks of „a flying forzando
able to close in .1 second" (2011). midi2exp and pianolatron gate the fast crescendo by hole
duration. None of these authors engages Hagmann on the point. Schmitz's sixth of the range per
minimum hole does not fit the six holes that must just reach the hook in control 4b and in his own
Bild 5, which put a step nearer a tenth. The „etwa" and the dependence on speed and starting
position leave room, and the tension is not resolved here.

The emulator treats the sforzando as acting per pulse. On roll 3309 the latched reading scores
0.151 / 0.192 of held-out error against 0.031 / 0.051 for the per-pulse one (`experiments.md`), the
punching re-triggers the on-line while a latch would already be set in 63 % and 39 % of cases, and
no rise continues after the port has closed (`measurements.md` §7). A latch that leaks, giving a
longer effective pulse, remains a possibility the fit could test through the effective pulse
length.

**The coupling to the crescendo.** Hagmann, p. 99: „mit der Position ‚Sforzando' stets auch die
Position ‚Crescendo' eingegeben", so that the bellows is also held closed through conduit 39, and
the cancel „erfolgt damit parallel über die Oeffnungen 11 und 9", with note 33 „Gründe für diese
Parallelschaltung sind nicht bekannt". In Hagmann's usage a „Position" is a glide-block opening
(pp. 106, 140) and „eingeben" is the roll delivering a command, including two lines punched
together (p. 127, „Schaltung"), and the relay has no access to the glide block. So this describes
a punching convention Hagmann observed, and the question is checkable on a roll. DRP 162708 has
„Mit Forte zugleich wird stets Crescendo beeinflußt", which in 1904 may have been wiring, and
Schmitz lists „crescendo + forzando f." as a basic movement of its own (1981, Bild 2). Roll 3309
does not pair the two codes, in either direction, above chance (`measurements.md` §6), and a relay
that set the crescendo with every sforzando would give one fast-crescendo rate rather than the two
the roll shows. `sforzandoSetsCrescendo` is kept as a flag and pinned at zero. The ablation prices
it at a small loss in the bass and nothing in the treble.

## 5. The Mezzoforte stop

**What it is.** The pin („Dorn", p. 97; „Haken", control 2c) of the closed Mezzofortebalg 93
interrupts „Oeffnung oder Schliessung des Nuancierbalgs auf halbem Wege" (Hagmann pp. 96–97). It
blocks in both directions. Hagemann 2001, p. 26, quoting Welte, has the Mezzofortebalg „versperrt
dem Nuancierbalg den Weg, sodaß solange jener zugesaugt ist, kein Ton stärker als Mezzoforte, oder
schwächer als Mezzoforte spielen kann". The Pianola Institute describes the movement as restricted
„either above or below a fixed mezzoforte level", and Pfeffer, ch. 1, P5 has the bellows confined
to the range between mezzoforte and fortissimo or between pianissimo and mezzoforte. Gottschewski's
name for the function is „Mezzofortearretierung" (1996, p. 138 n. 108), an arrest „zu
Steuerungszwecken" (p. 28). Brougher: „If the MF hook arrives just ahead of the pneumatic blade,
then it doesn't 'lock' the blade. It 'STOPS' the blade", and the hook is a right-angle, unpadded
blade (2011). In DRP 162708 stop *i* on bellows *h* „greift … in beliebig angeordnete Nasen am
Steuerungsbalge". Hagmann's verb is „unterbricht" and Anhang 13 shows no linkage between 93 and
90 other than the finger, so the stop cannot move the bellows. It confines it to the half it is
in, which is what `limitAtStop` does.

**Its position is regulated, not the printed midpoint.** Control 2c balances bass against treble
at mezzoforte by „Verstellung des Hakens an 93" (p. 181). On Anhang 13 the finger sits at about
53 % of the drawn travel from the open end, consistent with „auf halbem Wege" and worth no more
than that on a schematic. So `mezzoforte` is a free parameter per half. On roll 3309 the arrest
face lies at 0.627 / 0.645 of the travel between the rails, and a fit with the pin held at 0.5
scores 0.0424 / 0.0596 against 0.0335 / 0.0396 with it free (`measurements.md` §2,
`experiments.md`).

**Two arrest levels.** The Mezzofortebalg fixes the Nuancierbalg „mit seinem Anschlagstift in
zwei nah beieinander liegenden Lagen", the positions pp, ff „sowie zweimal mf (bedingt durch die
Materialstärke am Haken des Mezzofortebalges)" being the ones that can be held (Schmitz 1981,
p. 4, with Bild 2, which Gottschewski 1996, p. 29 n. 29 paraphrases). In Bild 2 movements rising
from pp stop at the lower level mf₁ and movements falling from ff at the upper level mf₂, which is
what a rigid hook of finite thickness gives, and Schmitz calls the diagram theoretical. On Anhang
13 and 14 the arm of 93 ends in a plain downward finger, an L with a blunt end and no notch, facing
a small pointed pin on the moving board of 90 (Schmitz's „Anschlagstift", the booklet's „Stift").
The two arrest levels are the two sides of the finger, and their separation at drawn line weight is
of the order of a tenth of the travel and is not a dimension. Roll 3309 has the order the other
way, the rising rests lying above the falling ones, which a stop that yields in the direction it is
pushed would give. That is a reading against Schmitz's sketch and rests on 13 and 7 rising rests
(`measurements.md` §2). `mfThickness` carries the separation and is pinned, since only the falling
face is measured. Schmitz calls the mezzoforte an auxiliary function that simplifies the punching
and absorbs accumulated error in valves that no longer work exactly (p. 7).

**Timing.** Conduit 54 carries no throttle on the drawing, where 39 and 23 each feed a numbered
adjuster, and Anhang 12 has no control timing the Mezzoforte bellows. Control 7 only checks that
it opens on release and that the still-set crescendo and sforzando then drive „rasch in die
Forte-Stellung" (p. 184). Welte normed every timing it cared about, so the emulator treats the
finger as instantaneous. Control 7 also has the finger catch a bellows travelling at sforzando
speed, so its descent is shorter than half the sforzando travel time. The Skala-Rolle 98 checks
only that the hook can return to rest while the pin is pressed against it (§7).

**Not covered by any source.** What happens when Mezzoforte is commanded while the bellows is
already past the midpoint (the geometry says the finger drops into free space and waits), and what
happens when the pin is exactly under the finger (a fouling window of about the finger's own width,
which the pointed pin may cam past). Snapping to mezzoforte within a small band is a numerical
choice, not a reading.

## 6. Downstream of the bellows

Everything here lies between the bellows and the hammer and cannot appear in a trace of bellows
travel. It is listed so that the omission is deliberate.

- **The Regelbalg 91** senses the delivered vacuum through a bore into the left half of chamber
  86 and, when it falls under dense playing, spring 101 opens the cone valve „so weit, bis die
  Gleichgewichtsstellung wieder erreicht ist" (Hagmann p. 97). Schmitz: „Somit wird unabhängig von
  der Anzahl der angeschlagenen Töne immer die gleiche Lautstärke erreicht" (1981, p. 3). Both
  bellows act on one chain band (p. 97), so the cone valve position is a sum of the two travels
  with both signs positive, and mezzoforte is a bellows position rather than a loudness. Schmitz
  gives the one statement of the ratio: „Eine Bewegung des Nuancierbalges um mehrere cm verlagert
  das Regelventil K nur um mehrere mm" (p. 3). The band is a chain and can only pull, so spring 89
  alone reseats the cone (this repository's reading of Anhang 13). US 1,008,291 has the regulating
  spring progressive, DRP 162708 the slide opening „sobald mehr Töne erklingen". The pianissimo
  level is set by the leather nut on the band (Hagmann p. 96, control 2a, Skala-Rolle 98 §2).
- **Throttle 97**, §3.
- **The Vorverstärker** (parts 102–111, p. 104, fed from the trunk) and the striking bellows 121,
  which closes „mehr oder minder rasch" with the vacuum (p. 105), form the map from vacuum to
  hammer speed. The Hammerleiste (holes 7/8) shifts the hammer rail independently (p. 105).
- **Both halves load one blower** (p. 96), so a loud left hand could slow the right hand's
  crescendo. The emulator passes the total note density to both halves for this reason. The roll
  shows no such effect (`measurements.md` §9).
- **Forte is a soft limit.** The bellows closes against its own spring, so a sagged vacuum may
  not close it fully (this repository's inference from p. 96).

## 7. The pedals

Both pedals are on/off pairs on the T-100 (Anhang 10, and Hagmann p. 106: lines 7 and 8 for the
Hammerleiste, 93 and 94 for the dampers), so both latch like Mezzoforte and Crescendo. The T-98
puts each on one line held for the duration (p. 106). Hagmann's note 48 (p. 107), „Cf. 114-116
(T 100), 118-120 (T 98)", refers to the pages of the Lang print, which land on the two relay
descriptions, so the pedal Vorpneumatik is the latching relay of §2.

**Dampers.** The latch feeds conduit 10 → throttle 11 → membrane chamber 12 → membrane 13 →
double valve 14, which seals atmospheric bore 15 and opens conduit 16 from blower conduit 7 into
bellows 18, which closes and lifts the dampers. On release air returns through 15 metered by
throttle 17, set so that opening takes „derselben zeitlichen Ausdehnung … wie seine Schliessung"
(Hagmann pp. 106–107). No adjuster sits on conduit 16. Control 9c sends a slow rise to throttle
11 and control 9b a slow fall to throttle 17 (p. 185). `src/model/pedal.ts` has the relay delay
(`relayLagMs`), the rise (`liftMs`) and the fall (`fallMs`) accordingly.

**Hammer rail.** „Auffallend einfach gebaut": bellows 19 on conduit 9 with the single throttle 20
for air and suction alike (p. 106). Control 8b adjusts 20. Hagmann's „Kontrolle B" (n. 49) is a
slip for 8, there being no lettered controls on the piano scale rolls (p. 109). Hagmann always
writes „verschieben" for what the rail does and never distinguishes grand from upright, and the
T-100's service life was mostly uprights and Vorsetzer (pp. 91–92), so the line means whatever the
host piano's left pedal does. MIDI CC 67 is the right destination because it is the soft-pedal
controller.

**No nuance, but travel time.** „Die Dämpfer werden stets in derselben Geschwindigkeit und immer
vollständig von den Saiten abgehoben", though half-pedal effects „durch Ueberlagerung der
verschiedenen vom Notenband ausgehenden Befehle" are to be suspected (Hagmann p. 112). So the
model has no depth control and one regulated speed per direction, and intermediate positions
arise only when the roll commands a change before the travel is finished. No source gives a
travel time. Control 10 is the one place the scale roll ties itself to seconds (16 repetitions
per note, „zirka 8 Töne pro Sekunde", p. 185), so a scan of a Monteur-Rolle could bound the pedal
speed from controls 9b, 9c and 10 on the same paper.

**Anhang 16 has the bass pair un-mirrored.** It draws the hammer-rail latch on line 7, which
Anhang 10 calls „Hammerleiste ab". Hagmann states the mirroring at p. 98 n. 28, and roll 3309
decides for Anhang 10: its damper punches alternate 93, 94 strictly over 533 punches and its
three hammer-rail punches read on, on, off under Anhang 10 and off, off, on under the sketch.

## 8. The drawn expression lines

**What they are said to record.** The documentary source is Schmitz. The rolls „die gemäß
Welte-Patent Nr. 412965 vom 30.8.21 die Lautstärkelinien enthalten" carry lines that „wurden auf
Kundenwunsch auf die fertig gestanzten Rollen aufgebracht, indem sie wieder abgespielt wurden und
zwei mit den Nuancierbälgen verbundene Zeichenstifte deren Bewegungsabläufe festhielten. Obwohl der
Mechanismus der Hebelübertragung unbekannt ist, ergeben diese Linien ein hinreichend genaues Bild
vom tatsächlichen Verlauf einzelner Funktionen" (1981, pp. 4–5). So on his account a line is the
record of one instrument playing the punched code, drawn onto the finished roll by a pen coupled
to the bellows through a linkage he does not know. The sentence is stated as fact and given no
source, and „hinreichend genau" is his judgement rather than a test. Gottschewski credits him
with the reading (2024, 08:01) and shows lines on WM1348, WM1474, WM1478, WM2739 and WM3357, none
of them 3309, with WM1478 (Samaroff, Grieg, punched 18 January 1923 against a copy of 30 April
1920) as his worked example. Pfeffer, ch. 1, n. 50 identifies the lines on 3309 as „Linien zur
Handnuancierung, die vermutlich unmittelbar aus einer aufgezeichneten Bewegung des Nuancierbalgs
stammen", the „vermutlich" marking a conjecture. Hagmann does not mention drawn lines on piano
rolls.

**What they were for.** GB 185,380, the British equivalent of the patent Schmitz cites, describes
a record with bass and treble zones bounded by parallel lines, „a middle line marked 'MF'", outer
lines „PP" and a central „FF", each zone carrying „a deviating expression line or graph", and an
expression control lever for each zone with a matching scale, so that when a person adjusts the
levers to the deviation of the lines „the desired strength of tone is obtained". A subsidiary
feature is „a peculiar zig-zag formation of the expression lines whereby they deviate to sharp
points", for readability. That is the printed header of the rolls with lines, „P.P. M.F. F.F.
M.F. P.P. / Bass. Discant / Handnuancierung" (Schmitz, Bild 3). The patent documents the layout
and purpose of the lines as a guide for two hand levers and says nothing about how they were
produced. Hagmann connects the same patent with the Mignola's hand levers of 1924 (p. 43), which
is this repository's inference from his text rather than his statement. Schmitz's account of the
pens and the patent are compatible, since a pen trace of the bellows is an obvious way to make such
a guide, but the claim that the lines record the bellows rests on his unsourced sentence and on the
behaviour of the lines themselves.

**The programme.** Slide 20 distinguishes the direct use of expression lines to evaluate the
encoding from the use of emulated lines, and the talk states the aim: „my idea was to emulate
expression lines by software. If the emulation proves to be very similar to existing expression
lines, emulated expression lines can be used for the evaluation of rolls that have no expression
lines" (09:01). His own emulation runs on the raw MIDI, is a work in progress and does not yet
emulate „the process of transition between different functions" (11:45). He gives no equations,
constants or code. This repository follows that programme on roll 3309.

**What he found in the lines.** They are not linear (§3). The instruments that drew them were
out of regulation: the length of the mezzoforte-to-pianissimo process differs from roll to roll
by more than paper speed explains and sometimes between left and right on one roll, „which should
not happen" (14:21–15:17), so he normalises them to a regulated instrument using a default setting
derived from several emulations (15:27). That is the same kind of observation as the bass/treble
disagreements found here, and he attributes it to regulation rather than to measurement.

**The lead.** The lines run ahead of their punches: „the fast crescendo caused by these forzando
forte holes begins a few millimeters too early in the expression lines. Perhaps there was a
technical reason that the lines could not be written exactly above the holes in the sliding block"
(11:01, 11:19), and he moves the holes to fit the lines for his demonstration. Pfeffer, ch. 1,
n. 50 observes the same offset and conjectures a reaction time („wohl um der menschlichen
Reaktionszeit bei der Handnuancierung Rechnung zu tragen"). Gottschewski 1996, p. 45 gives a
third, mechanical reason why dynamics on a finished roll must lead the notes: the recorder can
register the striking force at the earliest at the attack, while the reproducer must have reached
the level before it. On roll 3309 the offset differs by code and drifts along the roll
(`measurements.md` §5), which the emulator carries as `leadRows` and its variants and switches
off for playback.

**Why the lines exist.** Asked in Sydney, Gottschewski calls the surviving lines a feature for
customers on late rolls and the existence of a similar device in the editing room from the
earliest days a guess: „I guess, I don't know" (27:30–28:16). Welte did draw rolls before punching
them, on a Zeichenmaschine described by Hans Haaß (1927), whose paper take-up accelerated the
drawing board's feed. That workflow belongs to the mechanically produced light-music rolls of
about 1925–26 and does not transfer to a hand-played roll without argument (Gottschewski 1996,
p. 156). Welte's recording procedure is undocumented and no piano recording rolls survive (Hagmann
p. 70 n. 48, Gottschewski 1996, p. 41), the control tracks were edited after the fact (Gottschewski
1996, p. 40, item 6), and a roll punched in 1922 was „geändert nach Gieseking 29.2.24" (p. 35
n. 42). What apparatus drew the lines is stated by no source.

**Bellows position, cone valve, or vacuum.** From the mechanism, three quantities differ in what
they would show. A trace of bellows position carries no note-locked structure and no spike at a
cancel. A trace of the cone valve carries the Regelbalg's note-rate corrections. A trace of
delivered vacuum carries both and the dump through 97. Roll 3309 shows a large note-locked dip
that vanishes where the bellows is provably pinned, so the line records bellows position
(`measurements.md` §9).

## 9. Prior software

midi2exp and pianolatron share one model by descent. In velocity units, with red-Welte defaults
(`Expressionizer.h:108–111`, `Expressionizer.cpp:60–62`, `welte-red.js:10–20`):

```
welte_p 35   welte_mf 60   welte_f 90   welte_loud 75   left_adjust −5 (bass)
slow_decay_rate 2380 ms (p→mf)   fastC_decay_rate 300 ms (p→mf)   fastD_decay_rate 400 ms (f→p)
```

Rules, in order: the slow decay runs by default and is switched off, not superposed, whenever
slow crescendo, fast crescendo or fast decrescendo is active. Otherwise the active rates are
summed. With the hook on the curve may not cross `welte_mf` from the side it is on. Else with slow
crescendo on and fast crescendo off it is capped at `welte_loud`. Clamp to `[welte_p, welte_f]`.
Velocity is sampled at note onset, plus `left_adjust` in the bass. Mezzoforte and slow crescendo
latch from on-hole to off-hole, and the fast states are gated by hole duration. midi2exp integrates
per millisecond with the clamps applied every step. pianolatron evaluates only at control-hole
times and interpolates linearly between, so a saturation inside a segment is lost, worth 3 to 4
velocity units on a fast crescendo into the hook.

**Tracker extension.** Both lengthen hole ends, never starts, by `tracker_diameter × 0.75`
with `tracker_diameter = 1.413 mm × 300.25 px/in ÷ 25.4 = 16.7 px`: 13 ticks rounded in midi2exp
(applied to every note-off on every track, opt-in with `-a`), 12 ticks truncated in pianolatron
(sforzando and pedal off-holes only, always on). The emulator instead computes the open area of
the round port under the round punch row by row (`src/roll/aperture.ts`), which places the
opening about 11 ms earlier than the binary port with the tail extension.

**Acceleration.** Both write a linear 0.3147 ft/min² in 0.1-minute steps. In midi2exp it has no
effect on the velocities, because the time map is never invalidated after the tempo events are
written (`MidiFile.cpp:1312–1317`; verified: `--ac 0`, `0.3147` and `0.9` give identical
velocities on 9593 notes), so the curve is computed on the input file's own tempo map at a forced
division of 568. In pianolatron it does act, while the sample player may follow the file's own
tempo events, so velocities can land at times that do not match the curve.

**Provenance of the constants.** The 300 ms fast crescendo sits against a comment „test roll
shows around 170ms-200ms from min to MF hook", the 400 ms fast decrescendo against „166ms —
300ms". The 2380 ms slow decay carries no measurement, and 0.3147 and the roll tempo 94.6 carry no
source in either repository. The version history runs `fastC` 700 → 1050 → 300, `fastD` 330 →
400, `slow` 9520 → 4760 → 2380 (`Expressionizer.h:203–222`). No test roll is named. Brougher's
stopwatch timings are the other published figures: decrescendo from the hook to fully open 2.0 s,
crescendo from rest to the hook 2.5 s, a forzando pair on for 0.1 s reaching the hook (2011).

**Porting hazards.** midi2exp's `--sd`, `--fc`, `--fd` do nothing because the step rates are
computed once in `setupRedWelte`. A missing crescendo-off leaves the region unlatched (an explicit
`TODO` at `Expressionizer.cpp:1041`) where pianolatron latches to the end. A missing sforzando
note-off disables the hole in midi2exp and runs it to the end of the roll in pianolatron. Two
control holes at one tick give pianolatron a `0/0`.

**What neither represents**, and the emulator does: a flow law with a level-dependent rate, an
asymmetric fill and dump, a relay with threshold and tail, a stop with extent and compliance, a
regulated mezzoforte position, a crescendo asymptote instead of a cap, the port's finite opening,
per-code offsets of the drawn line. What neither represents and the emulator omits too: the
Regelbalg, throttle 97, the hammer rail's effect on velocity, and a map from bellows travel to
velocity, which is a property of the piano. On roll 3309 midi2exp's published constants score
0.387 / 0.359 against the drawn line, refitted 0.142 / 0.157, and the pneumatic model 0.034 / 0.040
(`experiments.md`, held-out blocks).
